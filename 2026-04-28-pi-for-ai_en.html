Pi’s Human Infrastructure for AI: 526 Million Tasks Completed by Distributed Workforce of 1 Million Humans

Published: April 28, 2026

Categories: general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

AI (artificial intelligence, computer systems that learn from data and make judgments) is advancing quickly. But the hardest part of building a reliable system is still deeply connected to humans. For companies that work to improve models, adjust the quality of inference (the step where a trained model gives an answer), and expand data labeling (the work of adding marks, such as correct answers, to data) and evaluation, human input is essential.

Building a strong model is not only a matter of adding more computing power. AI needs human-in-the-loop input (a way of working where humans take part inside the process) to improve outputs, define quality, check correctness, clear up unclear points, and make sure the system is truly useful to people.

Reinforcement learning without humans (a way for a system to learn by trying actions and receiving scores) and automated learning methods can work well in narrow areas or in clearly defined situations. They can help expand optimization (the work of finding the best settings) and raise efficiency. But they still have limits in important ways. They often optimize proxy measures (stand-in measures used in place of the real goal) instead of true human preferences. They are weak against reward hacking (using a loophole in the scoring system to raise only the apparent score). They also have difficulty fully capturing nuance (small differences in meaning), legitimacy (whether something is right and acceptable), changing norms (shared rules and expectations in society), and human judgment in the real world.

For that reason, no matter how much automated methods advance, human input remains essential for improving AI.

Practical challenges of human input in AI

The need for human input creates large practical challenges for AI companies.

  • Scale: AI companies need human input on a large scale. This is especially important in new fields such as robotics (technology for robots) and physical AI (AI that works with the physical world in real life). A future major advance may depend on foundation models (large basic models that can be adapted to many uses) trained on large amounts of human-made data about physical environments and interactions in the real world. In the same way that internet-scale data (data collected across the whole internet) was an important condition for the rise of large language models (AI models trained on very large amounts of text) such as ChatGPT, large-scale human data about the physical world may be an important condition for a similar major advance in robotics. Real people can provide this kind of data, including through digital or virtual environments. In those environments, human actions, movements, interactions with objects, movement from place to place, and task completion in space are recorded.
  • Being real: Large-scale human input is valuable only if it comes from real people and meets reliable quality standards. AI companies need ways to check identity, remove bots (programs that act automatically), and make sure answers are accurate, reliable, and useful. Without those protections, human-in-the-loop systems become weak against fraud, low-quality input, and weak learning signals (information used for learning).
  • Cost: High-quality and real human-in-the-loop systems cost a lot to build, run, and use. Companies need a place to put tasks, a system to gather participants, checks of contributors, ways to assign work, and a base that supports large-scale but flexible participation. They also need to pay for the labor itself in fiat currency (ordinary money issued by a government, such as dollars or yen). At a large scale, the burden of operations is not only the labor itself. The base, coordination, checks, and payment systems needed to make that labor usable are also a burden.

Proved at large scale: Pi Network's verified human workforce

Pi Network has already built a solution. This section introduces a large-scale workforce of identity-verified human participants who are active inside the Pi system and are spread across the world.

As one example of its scale and ability, more than 1 million verified individuals completed more than 526 million verification tasks on the network. These tasks were part of Pi's own KYC system (KYC, the process of checking a person's identity). The work of KYC verifiers was paid directly in Pi tokens (digital units of value issued on a blockchain). Unlike many other KYC tools, Pi's KYC combines automation by AI with a large-scale distributed human workforce in its own way. With this combination, it achieved accurate and efficient verification of more than 18 million people in more than 200 countries and regions. The more than 18 million identity-verified people may also take part in a marketplace of this kind of workforce (a place that connects jobs with workers).

Pi's solution creates a new base for AI and digital systems that need real, active, and ready-to-join human input for tasks from simple ones to tasks of medium difficulty. Because contributors are verified through KYC, companies that use Pi's distributed human workforce can lower the risk of contact with bots, fraud, and labor that cannot be verified. At the same time, they can meet important requirements for trust and for following the law from the start.

The meaning goes further. A global workforce has localization (adjustment to local languages, regions, and cultures) built in from the start across languages, regions, and cultural backgrounds. For that reason, it can create data, judgments, and feedback that are more closely related to products meant for real-world use. Also, unlike many alternatives in the market that do not have a substantial number of real humans, Pi's network, which has tens of millions of real people, has already shown that it can complete more than 500 million tasks and provide human input on a large scale. This means companies do not only gain access to labor. They also gain access to a human coordination base that can be measured.

Pi's payment and incentive base for distributed human work around the world

Large-scale human labor is useful only when millions of people can complete hundreds of millions of tasks efficiently, around the world, and with payment. By supporting rewards in Pi, or rewards in a company's own token through Pi Launchpad (a system for issuing tokens for new projects on Pi), Pi Network's model opens a new way to align work, motivation, and growth of the system. This is important at a time when traditional fiat currency models may no longer fit global and flexible participation in units of work.

Global payment base: Paying millions of people in fiat currency across countries and regions can create large friction (difficulty and extra steps) in payment processing, transfers across borders, following the law, and handling very small payments. Pi already has a base that can make this layer of moving payments simple, and a distribution system that uses blockchain (a technology that keeps records of transactions in a shared form that is hard to change). In addition, Pi's workers already have a valid Pi wallet (a place to hold and send digital money), so friction in joining is lower, and there is no need to teach users a new payment system.

Cost efficiency: Payment in Pi may have a cost advantage over many fiat currency systems, because it can lower fees in the middle, friction in payments across borders, work for banks and payments, and extra costs for small payments. This may also be an advantage compared with a base such as Mechanical Turk (a service where companies ask many people to do small tasks), where a fee for the requester is added on top of payment to workers.

Launchpad tokens as a tool for business models: Companies can also pay contributors with their own token on Pi Mainnet (the main, live network) through Pi Launchpad. Pi Launchpad is now being improved again and again on Testnet (a network for testing). This is part of Pi's work on new business models that are made possible by blockchain and are made for the AI age. A token is not only a means of payment. It is designed for gaining users and for practical use of a product, and it is connected to real use. A Pi Launchpad token can lower a company's costs by supporting rewards, participation, user growth, and involvement in the system with tokens, instead of paying for all of it in cash. For that reason, payment becomes part of a wider growth strategy, not only an operating cost.

A token also works as a tool to keep a connection with people who complete work and receive a reward. Those people may become users of a company whose service they helped to build through their contribution. A token can also be built into the company's own product as payment, a discount on services offered, access, governance (a system for taking part in how something is run), or another way to take part. For a company, issuing such a token can also mean having another liquid asset (an asset that can be exchanged or used easily) at hand for business needs. Unlike the common way of treating tokens in Web3 (the idea of a decentralized web based on blockchain), Pi Launchpad places tokens as practical tools connected to working apps and real use, not as speculative assets for raising funds (assets bought or sold mainly in the hope that the price will rise).

AI does not only change how people live and work. It also requires new business models for companies to survive, grow, and succeed.
