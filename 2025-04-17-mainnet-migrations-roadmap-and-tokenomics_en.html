Mainnet Migrations Roadmap By Priorities & Explaining Pi’s Tokenomics and Supply

Published: April 17, 2025

Categories: general, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

This article sets out the path for Mainnet (the live network) migration based on the network's priorities, and makes Pi's tokenomics (the system for the supply and allocation of tokens) and supply mechanics clear.

The Mainnet migration path by priority

When discussing Pi Mainnet migration, the following facts need to be kept in mind. (1) Migration has to be done for a network of tens of millions of people, based on complex mining data from the past six years. It must ensure accuracy, safety, and fairness for honest Pioneers, while removing fraud. It is not a simple airdrop (free distribution) like those of many other crypto projects, which simply distribute to tens of thousands of wallets without thinking much about extra data. (2) The network has already migrated more than 12 million people. That in itself is an achievement of scale in the industry, especially because it was done through a KYC (identity check) and migration process built by Pi itself, with no cost in legal currency (government-issued money) for users. (3) When the migration condition for moving to the Open Network (the live network connected to the outside) was set at 10 million people, it balanced the network's need to move to the Open Network at the right time with including millions of Pioneers on the Mainnet. It was understood and expected that some people would still need to migrate after the Open Network started.

The path for the Mainnet migration work, based on the network's priorities, is as follows.

  • Complete the first migrations of Pioneers in the queue: the network is currently completing the first migrations of Pioneers in the queue. These include verified base mining rewards, verified security circle rewards (a system where you register people you trust), lockup rewards (rewards for setting Pi aside so it cannot be used for a set period), utility app use rewards, and verified Node rewards (rewards for computers that support the network).
  • Second migrations, including referral bonuses: after the first migrations of the current queue are complete, second migrations become the focus. Second migrations also include referral mining bonuses that belong to referral team members who have passed KYC.
  • Ongoing regular migrations: finally, Pi Network will move to regular migrations — for example monthly or quarterly. The frequency will be decided later, and all bonuses and rewards will be included.

Note that the difference between the transferable balance shown on screen and the balance actually migrated is intended. To save calculation time and resources, the screen uses an estimate from a simplified calculation. The balance actually migrated to a user's Mainnet wallet depends on an extensive, accurate calculation across all the mining sessions (each round of mining) over the past years and the bonuses that came with them, and it can take much longer for each user. For that reason, the transferable balance shown on screen is a conservative estimate of the actual amount of Pi included in the first migration.

For everyone, the actual first migration already includes verified base mining rewards, security circle rewards, lockup rewards, utility app use rewards, and, for some people, verified Node rewards. Basically, only the referral mining bonuses are excluded.

Pi's tokenomics and supply

Following the discussion of migration, the article explains Pi's tokenomics and mining mechanics, which were defined at length in the whitepaper (a document that explains a project's plan and system) in 2021, to remind Pioneers and to inform the general public, because this is important.

About Pi's supply and allocation

Pi's maximum supply is 100 billion tokens. The maximum supply is made up of the following.

  • 65 billion tokens (65%): allocated to all community mining rewards.
  • 10 billion tokens (10%): allocated to the foundation reserve.
  • 5 billion tokens (5%): allocated for liquidity purposes (to make buying and selling easier).
  • 20 billion tokens (20%): allocated to the Core Team (the central development team).

Each allocation above is tied to the progress of issuance of the community's migrated mining rewards. So the share of each allocation in the total supply stays the same at any point in time.

Pi's effective total supply — that is, the total supply of Pi at the present time — is allocated in the same proportions as the maximum supply. Because every allocation is tied to the community's migrated mining rewards, the effective total supply can be calculated by dividing the migrated mining rewards currently on the Mainnet blockchain by 65%. The other allocations within the effective total supply can be calculated based on the same proportions as the maximum supply. For example, up to 10% of the effective total supply can be used as the foundation reserve, 5% for liquidity purposes, and 20% by the Core Team. This does not change even though all tokens were technically required by the blockchain's rules and were issued at genesis (the very beginning).

This allocation structure itself ensures the neutral role of every process that affects the speed of migration. This is because every other allocation category is tied to the progress of migrated mining rewards. It is designed, on purpose, to align the interests of all parties in the network toward putting as many Pioneers and as much Pi as possible onto the Mainnet. For that reason, the network is putting its effort into migrating Pioneers and their Pi as quickly as possible.

How Pi mining works

Pi's mining mechanics are designed to broaden growth, inclusiveness (welcoming many people without distinction), and safety, in addition to decentralization, utility, stability, and long-term duration.

Pi mining rewards are distributed based on an issuance formula that follows a declining exponential decay model (a type of formula where the decrease becomes gentler over time), as defined in the Pi whitepaper. Pioneers can increase the amount of mining rewards they receive based on their individual contributions to the network, such as security circles, use of utility Pi apps, and operating a Node. For each month, the amount of Pi distributed as mobile balances is capped and set by the model, no matter how many people there are that month or how many kinds of mining rewards there are. The cap is achieved through the design of the system-wide base mining rate, and each type of mining reward for each individual is only a multiple of this base mining rate. Because the monthly supply always decreases, the base mining rate generally goes down over time.

Summary

The network is committed to completing Mainnet migration as quickly as possible for all genuine, verified, and honest Pioneers, while ensuring the network's accuracy, safety, and integrity. Pi's tokenomics are designed to align long-term network growth with real contributions from the community, and to make sure the issuance system stays fair, based on progress, and rooted in real participation. Readers are guided to read the Pi whitepaper for details.
