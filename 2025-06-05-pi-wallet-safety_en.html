Protect Your Pi Wallet: Critical Safety Reminders

Published: June 5, 2025

Categories: safety

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

In the Open Network (the production network that can connect to the outside), giving priority to the safety of the self-custody Pi Wallet and of Pi is more important than ever. Because a blockchain cannot be changed, a mistake such as sharing your wallet passphrase with a malicious person or a phishing site (a trick using fake screens and the like) can lead to transactions that cannot be undone. For that reason, taking the right precautions now is essential.

You must not engage at all with any website, app, or platform inside Pi Browser that asks you to enter your wallet passphrase and is not exactly "wallet.pinet.com". Doing so can put your entire Pi balance in danger that cannot be recovered. Pi Browser is a browser, like other browsers such as Chrome and Safari, and in the end, it is up to each individual Pioneer to judge the safety and legitimacy of the sites and URLs they visit.

So that Pioneers can tell a fake wallet apart, the real Pi Wallet is also identified visually, in purple, in the Pi Browser navigation bar, and it shows the Pi logo featuring the Core Team app logo. Even if a scammer creates a fake "Pi Wallet" to trick Pioneers out of their secret wallet passphrase, they cannot change the official Pi Wallet's app URL or the browser-related screens.

On social media, be especially careful about fake websites, misleading advertisements (such as advertisements to fake websites), fake email addresses, and other places where malicious people can give Pioneers a wrong impression and wrong information.

Your wallet passphrase is private and must never be shared. Members of the Pi Core Team will never ask users for any authentication account information, such as a wallet passphrase, an account password, or a verification code. Members of the Pi Core Team will also never phone individual Pioneers, and Pioneers must not submit information on platforms, websites, or apps that are not listed as official Pi apps and products in the Pi Safety Center.

For the two-factor authentication (2FA, a system that adds one more step of confirmation on top of the usual one) confirmation needed to confirm a migration wallet, trust only 2FA emails sent from an official Pi Network domain, such as [email protected]. Note that this email address is shown masked in the original text, and the exact characters cannot be read from the text.

For confirmed information, official links and domains, and past safety announcements, refer to the Pi Safety Center. It is the best place to get information and stay protected.
