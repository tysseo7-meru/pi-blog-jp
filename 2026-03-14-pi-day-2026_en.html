Happy Pi Day 2026: Major Feature and Ecosystem Releases Supporting Pioneers and Developers

Published: March 14, 2026

Categories: announcement, pi-day

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Day 2026 is the seventh official anniversary of Pi Network. It reflects years of new ideas and community building across the whole system.

From the start, Pi has been built following Pi's long-term vision and plan. The goal is to create a cryptocurrency (digital money that uses computer networks) that anyone can join, that focuses on usefulness, that many people use, and that many people can access. As the network keeps moving in this direction, Pi Day is a chance to introduce new abilities that make the base of the system stronger and give Pioneers (Pi users) and developers more ways to help and take part.

Today, Pi introduces a group of new features and products. They are designed to spread the creation of usefulness and deepen involvement across the Pi system. The updates include the release of the Pi Launchpad MVP version (MVP means a first working version with the smallest set of useful features) on Testnet (a test network) following one design from the recently introduced PiRC. This lets Pioneers learn and understand the new system by seeing it work. The updates also include progress in node and protocol upgrades, second migrations, rewards for KYC validators, and new abilities such as Mainnet Pi payments in Pi App Studio to increase usefulness. The releases also include a new external connection that widens access to Pi, and a new challenge focused on usefulness to encourage Pioneers to build and explore in the Pi system.

The sections below give details on each of these releases and how each one helps to spread usefulness and participation across the Pi system.

Pi Launchpad Released on Testnet

Pi has repeatedly worked on the design of ecosystem tokens (tokens made for use inside the Pi system) on Mainnet (the main live network), which was released on the first anniversary of Open Network. The ecosystem token layer is important for the Pi system, and Pi Launchpad is the main way to put good-quality ecosystem tokens that fit Pi's ideas onto Mainnet. Because of this, a Pi Request for Comment (PiRC, a proposal document that asks the community for opinions) was published to ask the community for feedback.

Now, an early version of Pi Launchpad that uses the system from Design 1 in the PiRC has been released as a Pi app on Testnet, together with a test token. Launchpad introduces a new idea for many Pioneers and uses a system that is different from common token launches in Web3 (the idea of a web based on blockchain networks). For this reason, it was first introduced on Testnet so the community can get used to how the process works before the Mainnet launch. This is also a learning process. Pioneers can learn the new Launchpad system and many DeFi (decentralized finance, financial services without a central company in control) processes by seeing them work, even if they have not read the PiRC on GitHub. Data and opinions collected during this period will be used to improve the product before the Mainnet version is introduced. You can access the Pi Launchpad app through Pi Browser.

As introduced at the Open Network anniversary, ecosystem tokens launched by Pi Launchpad are designed for getting users and for product usefulness, not mainly for raising funds. In this form, projects issue tokens as a tool to get users for their apps, and they build the tokens directly into the product's functions. For example, the tokens can be used for access, payments, rewards, or governance (how a project is managed by voting and rules) inside the app. The Pi income from a token launch does not go to the project that issued the token. Instead, it goes into a liquidity pool (a pool where assets are kept together to make trading smooth) with the ecosystem token. This creates a healthy base for liquidity (how easily an asset can be traded) from the start.

For projects, an ecosystem token is a system to attract real users, collect opinions, check the product, and improve it again and again. Without a token, which is a Web3 tool, getting users usually costs a lot of money, and the difficulty at the early stage of starting is large, especially for new companies. The Pi system provides these tools and support to businesses that want to show new products to a large global group of users. The users include both people who know a lot about crypto assets and general users, and many of them have finished identity checks. At the same time, the system invites users to join the journey of improving and developing the product. By linking tokens to a real product experience, projects can not only create usefulness for their own token, but also involve users from an early stage of development, let them test product features and functions, check whether the product meets real human needs, and grow the product more effectively.

From the Pioneers' point of view, Launchpad gives clear and fair access to these tokens, based on merit (what a project or person has actually done). Pioneers can choose to take part in different projects, access tokens through the launch system, and use those tokens inside the products. This creates a cycle where users interact with a product, judge its usefulness, and ask projects to provide useful services. Unlike Web2 products (products on the traditional web), users do not only use products provided by a company. They also hold and use tokens issued by the company from an early stage.

Projects that launch through Pi Launchpad must put the product first. This means a project must already have a working app, so the token has usefulness right away at the time of launch.

The Launchpad system also supports the progress of activity on a decentralized exchange (DEX, an exchange where users trade directly without a central company) inside the Pi system. The Pi DEX now exists on Testnet, but a healthy trading environment depends on whether proper tokens with real usefulness and liquidity are available. By letting product-first projects launch ecosystem tokens and by sending the Pi income into a liquidity pool with those tokens, Pi Launchpad is intended to help create a flow of tokens with these features for use on Pi DEX, and to meet the need for liquidity. This structure can help prevent early misuse on Mainnet, where the DEX and AMM (automated market maker, a system that sets prices and allows trades automatically) might otherwise be filled with speculative tokens (tokens bought only in hope of a price rise) that have no meaningful usefulness. With this method, tokens that fit Pi's focus on usefulness can be created inside the system, and more active trading can be supported while meeting the goal of providing a safe place for Pioneers to take part.

For details on the original design ideas behind ecosystem tokens on Mainnet, you are guided to read the Open Network anniversary article, watch the video by Pi founder Chengdiao Fan, and read the detailed design proposal on GitHub and give your opinion.

With the Pi Day product release, in addition to reading materials and watching videos, you can try the first version of Pi Launchpad and learn the system by seeing it work. Please note that the Launchpad product may be improved quickly and may change during the test stage. For example, the length of each launch stage, whether different steps can be used, what information is available, and how calculations are done may change. The content may not be the same each time you visit. This fast change and inconsistency will not happen in the Mainnet version. The fast improvement and change in the Testnet version are intended as part of product testing, and the results will be reflected in the design to reach the best version on Mainnet.

Also, the overall design of ecosystem tokens and the Pi Launchpad product may change based on community opinions on the design, data collected during the Testnet period, and opinions from projects that intend to issue such utility tokens (tokens made to be useful in a product).

Node and Protocol Upgrades

All major Pi nodes (computers that help run the network) are now upgraded to version 20.2 and support Protocol 20. Pi plans to complete the upgrade of the Mainnet blockchain (a shared record of transactions kept on many computers) to Protocol 20 within the coming week. This is part of the work to align the Pi blockchain with the newest protocol version.

Protocol upgrades have been done step by step through consecutive versions.

  • The Testnet1 blockchain was upgraded step by step in 2025 and reached v23 on September 18, 2025. This blockchain was upgraded first, and the upgrade process was tested.
  • The Mainnet and Testnet2 blockchains were upgraded in the past month: v19.6 on February 15, 2026, v19.9 on March 1, 2026, and v20.2 on March 13, 2026.

On August 25, 2025, Pi also released a Linux CLI version (a version operated by typed commands) of Pi Node, so Linux node operators can more easily manage Docker containers (standard boxes that hold programs and their settings) and database migrations (moving data from one database system to another).

Protocol 20 provides the base that makes the power of smart contracts (programmable processes that run on a blockchain) possible. With smart contracts, developers can build decentralized apps (apps that do not rely on one central server) and automate transactions according to conditions set in advance. This power can support a wide range of blockchain-based functions, such as issuing and managing NFTs (non-fungible tokens, digital assets that cannot be replaced by an identical copy), making marketplace transactions on the chain possible, and app processes that run automatically when certain conditions are met. With this protocol upgrade, the Pi blockchain now includes the technical base needed to support these programmable interactions inside the system.

In addition to the protocol upgrades needed to make the power of smart contracts possible, the rollout of smart contracts will be done step by step, and types that fit utility-based product innovation and operations will be given priority. The specific types of smart contracts that are featured will depend greatly on needs that arise from the process of creating usefulness.

For example, subscriptions are a typical business operation that happens in many real products. For this reason, types of smart contracts that make subscription functions possible will be given priority on the Pi blockchain. This priority reflects the goal of the Pi system: to make real products possible that have utility-based business models and solve real problems, rather than focusing only on transaction-centered or DeFi financial systems. This is the Pi strategy discussed at the Open Network anniversary.

In addition to contracts related to subscriptions, escrow-type smart contracts (contracts that hold assets for a short time during a trade), NFT-related contracts, and other utility-focused smart contracts will also be given priority. Some of these contracts are now being checked by external audits (checks by outside experts).

The process of introducing these smart contracts generally follows several stages. First, they are checked by an external audit service. Next, they are released to the community for checking and for collecting opinions. After that stage, the contracts are placed on Testnet for testing. When testing is complete and the contracts work as expected, they will be placed on Mainnet.

At the same time, other base parts will also be updated to handle the power of smart contracts. For example, changes will be made to Pi Wallet and Pi SDK (a set of development parts for building apps) so the wider base of the system can support and include smart contract functions and provide a smooth user experience.

Second Migration and Referral Bonus Migration

After much expectation from the community, Pi Network announced that second migrations have started and will continue with a step-by-step rollout. This opens a way for Pioneers to move additional Pi to Mainnet and take part further in the system. Pioneers who have already migrated their Pi balance in the first migration may be eligible to migrate their second transferable Pi balance.

For both the first and second migrations, before becoming fully eligible, Pioneers must set up two-factor authentication (2FA, a second check to confirm identity, in addition to a password) for Pi Wallet through Step 3 of the Mainnet Checklist. This is an important step to make the wallet safer. This may include adding a trusted email address to the account if such an email address has not yet been set for the account. 2FA is required before migration because transfers on a blockchain cannot be cancelled and cannot be changed.

As stated in the Mainnet migration plan, second migrations will also include referral mining bonuses based on Referral Team members having fully passed KYC (Know Your Customer, checking a person's identity). For this reason, Pioneers need to encourage their Referral Team to complete KYC. By doing so, these bonuses can be migrated.

While second migrations are rolled out, first migrations for eligible Pioneers will continue as normal. After the network successfully completes first and second migrations for the current queue of eligible Pioneers, Pi plans to start regular periodic migrations on an ongoing basis, with the frequency to be decided in the future.

Rewards for KYC Validators

Pi will release the first round of KYC validator rewards. The rewards will be distributed over a period of more than 10 days from now.

The original design from when the KYC app started several years ago is shown again. Each Pioneer pays 1 Pi when they migrate to Mainnet, into the pool that is the source of rewards to be distributed to human KYC validators. The work of KYC validators should be paid from this pool as long as it contributes to the accuracy of application processing, whether the final KYC result of the application is pass or fail. This is because correctly judging a fail is also a contribution to the network. However, a person whose KYC application failed does not migrate to Mainnet, so they cannot pay the 1 Pi.

For this reason, Pi's validator rewards pool is distributed evenly to all eligible work done within that period. The price per validation is decided by dividing the number of Pi in the pool by the total number of successful validations. A KYC validator's reward is this price multiplied by the number of validations that validator completed successfully. The first round of rewards was calculated by taking a snapshot (a record of the state at one point in time) of the pool and the total number of successful work before distribution. That point in time is up to March 5, 2026. New Pi entering the pool and new successful validations will be handled in the second and later distributions.

Following the method above, at the time of the snapshot, there were 16,568,774 Pi in the validator rewards pool. This is because 16,568,774 Pioneers had migrated to Mainnet. Also, the number of successful human validations was 526,970,631, and the number of human validators who did the work was 1,094,680.

The period covered by the first round of validator rewards is a time when the network of KYC validators was still in its starting stage. Many validations in that period were used to train human validators so they could do later validation work skillfully and accurately. This early stage also contributed to forming the KYC system itself, including establishing and expanding the workforce of human validators. For these reasons, Pi Foundation decided to contribute 10 million Pi to the pool to supplement the first distribution.

As a result, the final calculation of the price per validation in the first round is as follows: (16,568,774 Pi + 10 million Pi) ÷ 526,970,631 successful validations = 0.0504179 Pi per validation. This price is 21 times the current base mining rate.

To receive rewards in this first round, a validator needed to have completed at least 50 validations that reached majority agreement by March 5, 2026. Payment will be sent automatically to the validator's Mainnet Pi wallet, so validators need to have a Mainnet wallet ready and confirmed in the Mainnet Checklist. A detailed breakdown of validator rewards and performance status can be checked in the validator dashboard inside the KYC app.

Each Pioneer's KYC application has needed an average of 20 validations before a final decision. This is because the KYC system is designed to protect privacy, and it splits one application into several different validation tasks, and each task needs 2 validations by at least 2 different validators. The tasks include liveness checks (checks that a person is alive and present), document checks, photo matching, data matching, name checks, and others. For applications with special requests such as name updates, or applications that need extra checks such as multiple liveness checks or resubmissions, the number of validations may increase.

The unit price of rewards may change for each distribution round in each period, depending on factors such as the number of Pioneers who migrated and the total amount in the pool from validated reviews in that period. Also, as AI processing in KYC increases and validations for the purpose of training the early workforce decrease, fewer human validations will be needed per application. For this reason, the total number of validations will decrease and the rewards pool will be shared in that form, so in calculation, the price per validation may rise in future rounds. In future distributions, additional criteria or different criteria based on a validator's consistency and accuracy may also be introduced.

Separate from the distribution of KYC validator rewards, the data above shows several large achievements of the Pi community. Not only have more than 16.5 million people completed KYC through this KYC system and migrated to Mainnet, but the scale, consistency, and coordination of Pi community's large and distributed human workforce have shown that it can produce meaningful real-world results at a global scale that need human judgment and input. This not only honors the effort of validators in the KYC process, but also shows the possibility that the Pi community can contribute to other types of work where humans are needed for the network and the future AI economy, such as many human-in-the-loop processes in AI (processes where humans check or help AI work) and the human input needed to train and improve AI abilities.

Many AI projects talk about the importance of humans and human input in such systems and have built bases to make human input possible, but they face a large problem that there are no humans actually taking part, or only a few. Pi solved the difficult problem first. Pi already has a large and active network and has shown the will and ability to contribute. In addition, there is Pi's unique global payment system through a blockchain that can handle payments to a workforce at a large scale. For example, in just one app, 526,970,631 tasks were accomplished by 1,094,680 people, and the rewards are paid in Pi through the Pi blockchain. The KYC validator workforce and its results have built the base for future human-in-the-loop products, both at the base level and through third-party apps. This widens the ways humans can contribute to the creation and improvement of AI and the future economy. Details on these wider meanings are planned to be shared in a later announcement.

Pi App Studio Apps Now Work on Mainnet

Pi App Studio now supports Mainnet apps and the inclusion of in-app payments on the Mainnet blockchain. With this, some eligible App Studio apps can move from Testnet to Mainnet if they meet the standards for Mainnet apps, and they can build real Pi transactions directly into the user experience.

Four apps were invited to move to Mainnet and to include real Pi payments. The eligible criteria are planned to be published at a later date, but they generally follow the standards of Pi's Mainnet Ecosystem Interface. Those standards include app quality, completeness of app functions, evaluation of usefulness, and fit with Pi system policies and guidelines.

This milestone shows an important change for Pi App Studio, from experiment and testing to sustainable usefulness. One of Pi's long-term goals is to bring meaningful production activity onto the chain, especially in the layer of AI apps. Making Mainnet migration possible inside App Studio means that apps made with the help of AI by creators who are not technical experts do not have to stay as test prototypes (early test versions). They can take part in real on-chain activity.

This power is especially strong because Pi has a large base of real participants. Creators who build apps in App Studio can reach an existing system with more than 17.7 million Pioneers who have completed KYC on Mainnet. Those Pioneers can pay with real Pi that is transacted on the blockchain. In addition, through Pi's mobile base, creators can reach Pioneers at a scale of several more millions. This creates an environment that is fundamentally different from many other bases, where creators often struggle to find a place to distribute their apps or an active user base.

For this reason, Pi App Studio provides a base for a sustainable business model for creators. Creators can earn real Pi when their apps provide real usefulness and useful services to Pioneers. This encourages the development of higher-quality, more useful, and more attractive apps. Over time, this movement strengthens the usefulness layer of the system and makes it possible for apps to continue, improve, and expand in scale in order to create real benefits for both users and creators.

Persistent payment inclusion (payment settings that stay valid over time) is also now possible. As part of this release, App Studio creators on Testnet and Mainnet can include payment interactions in newly created apps that apply persistently across multiple uses. This is an improvement over the previous version. Previously, payment interactions applied only within one use while the app was in use.

With persistent payment interactions, purchases and unlocks stay valid beyond one use and continue to apply when a user returns to the app later. For example, a user can purchase access to a paid feature, unlock additional power, or get a lasting upgrade that can be used in future uses as well. This lets creators design more lasting in-app experiences that continue beyond one-time interactions.

Support on Kraken

Third-party services that have passed Pi's KYB verification (Know Your Business, checking the identity and business of a company) can connect with Pi Network and help with connections to external systems and the wider crypto asset system. Kraken, a centralized exchange (an exchange run by one central company), has included support for Pi.

Pi Day 2026 Utility Challenge

To celebrate Pi Day 2026 and the many new powers being released across the system, Pi introduces a challenge for Pioneers that focuses on usefulness.

This challenge is a checklist of simple activities that guide Pioneers to different parts of the system. Each task is linked to Pi usefulness or a newly released feature. For example, exploring apps, learning about new products, or interacting with tools available in the network. By completing these activities, Pioneers can learn how different parts of the system work together and experience the latest progress in practice.

When all tasks in the checklist are completed, Pioneers receive a Pi Day badge. This badge is shown on Pi Chats and Pi Social Profiles. This challenge is designed to make exploring the Pi system more interactive, while showing the spread of usefulness available to the community.

Open Network Anniversary Raffle Ends

The raffle for Pi goods to celebrate the Open Network anniversary, which started from the community badge effort, will end at 11:59 PM on March 14 (PST, Pacific Standard Time). Pioneers who received eligible badges before the deadline and submitted social media posts through the in-app flow will be eligible for the raffle. A total of 150 winners will be chosen at random and will be contacted only from an official email address, together with detailed guidance on receiving the goods.

When Pioneers receive an email about the raffle, they need to pay attention to the sender's email address and check the Pi Safety Center as guidance to avoid fraud.

Looking Ahead

The Pi Day releases this time show a further milestone in the continued evolution of the Pi system. By expanding the base, making new forms of participation possible, and supporting the creation of real usefulness, these updates strengthen the whole system. As more Pioneers migrate to Mainnet, build apps, and engage with usefulness, the network will continue to support Pi's cryptocurrency system that is widely used and focuses on usefulness.
