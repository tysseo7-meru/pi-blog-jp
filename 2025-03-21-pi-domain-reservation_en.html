.pi Domain Reservation For Compliant Pi Community Apps That Completed PiNet Migration

Published: March 21, 2025

Categories: developer, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

As part of the .pi domain auction (a sale where people bid to buy a name), Pi wants compliant Pi community apps that have completed PiNet migration (moving an app to work through PiNet, Pi's system for opening apps outside the Pi app) to be able to secure their domains without bidding. This follows the PiNet migration process announced earlier and the messages shared on Pi Day in 2024.

If an app has already completed PiNet domain migration and integration (connecting the app to PiNet) and follows the Ecosystem listing guidelines (the rules for being listed in the ecosystem), its matching .pi domain is reserved for that app and will not be included in the auction.

Some developers, including new developers, may have missed the first chance to move to PiNet, even though there were several messages about it during 2023 and 2024. To deal with this, Pi is giving eligible Pi apps that are on the Ecosystem listing a grace period (extra time to meet the rules) until May 28 to complete PiNet integration and secure their .pi domain.

To have an app's .pi domain reserved and taken out of the auction, the app must fully complete PiNet integration and follow the Ecosystem listing guidelines by May 28. Both requirements must be met before the deadline.

The next steps for developers are as follows.

  • Check whether your app is already integrated with PiNet. If you have completed PiNet migration, no further action is needed, and your domain is reserved.
  • If your app is not yet integrated with PiNet: complete PiNet integration by May 28 to secure your domain. You can find guidance on PiNet setup in the PiNet Developer Guide (a guide for developers).
  • If you choose not to migrate to PiNet, your domain will stay in the auction and will not be reserved automatically.

Pi asks people to get in touch if they have questions or need help. Pi says it is thankful for contributions to the ecosystem and looks forward to developers continuing to create new things.

How to set up PiNet

  • Register your PiNet URL: First, you must register your PiNet URL in the Pi Developer Portal (the entry point for developers). You can find this field on the "PiNet Settings" page in the app dashboard (the screen for managing your app). When you open this page, you must first register the URL.
  • Choose how the app is shown: Choose how users will see your app. The choices are fullscreen (using the whole screen) or mobile screen. If a developer selects the fullscreen checkbox, a user who opens the app in a desktop browser will see the app spread across the whole window.
  • Understand the safety feature added to the URL: A random string of characters will be added to the first URL. This is a safety feature to prevent impersonation (pretending to be someone else) and domain squatting (registering a domain name early to take it before others can use it). High-quality Pi apps may be given a plain URL without the unique string added, at the discretion of the Pi Core Team (the central development team).
  • Check that the URL was created: Once the URL is registered, the app should become available at the PiNet URL shown in the app dashboard. However, this is not the end.
  • Make your app work well with PiNet: It is best practice (the recommended way of doing something) to make your app work well for PiNet use. Developers should update the user flow (the steps a user goes through in the app) so that people who are not Pioneers (Pi users) also have the chance to experience as much of the app as possible.
