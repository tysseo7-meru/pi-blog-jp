Pi Launchpad Released on Testnet

Published: March 19, 2026

Categories: announcement, pi-day

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

This article was originally part of the Pi Day 2026 announcements. A walkthrough video that broadly introduces the Launchpad's design and screens has now been added.

Pi has repeatedly refined the design of ecosystem tokens (tokens used within the Pi ecosystem) on Mainnet, which was released at the first anniversary of the Open Network. Because the ecosystem token layer is important for the Pi ecosystem, and because Pi Launchpad (a system that supports the release of new tokens) is the main way to put high-quality ecosystem tokens that fit Pi's approach onto Mainnet, a Pi Request for Comment (PiRC, a proposal document that asks for comments) was published to gather opinions from the community.

Now, an early version of Pi Launchpad that uses the PiRC Design 1 system has been released, together with test tokens, as a Pi app on the testnet (the test network used before production). The Launchpad brings in ideas that are new to many Pioneers, and it uses a system that differs from the token releases common in Web3 (the decentralized web built on blockchains). For that reason, it was first introduced on the testnet, before the Mainnet release, so that the community can get used to how the process works. Even for people who have not read the PiRC on GitHub, it is also an educational process where Pioneers can learn the new Launchpad system and many DeFi (decentralized finance, financial systems run on blockchains) processes in actual operation. The data and feedback collected during this period will be used to refine and improve the product before the Mainnet version is introduced. The Pi Launchpad app can be accessed from Pi Browser.

As introduced at the Open Network anniversary, the ecosystem tokens released through Pi Launchpad are designed mainly for user acquisition and product usefulness, not for raising funds. In this form, a project issues a token as a tool to gain users for its own app, and builds the token directly into the product's functions. For example, it can be used for access within the app, payments, rewards, and governance (the system for taking part in how something is run). The Pi proceeds gained from the token release do not go to the issuing project. Instead, they are put into a liquidity pool (a system that keeps assets together in one place to make trading smooth) with the ecosystem token, creating a healthy base of liquidity (ease of trading) from the start.

For projects, an ecosystem token becomes a system to attract real users, gather feedback, and check and improve the product repeatedly. Without the Web3 tool of tokens, gaining users usually costs a lot of money, and for startups in particular, the early-stage difficulty of not having the first users gather is large. The Pi ecosystem provides such tools and support to businesses that want to show innovative products to a large worldwide group of users. The users include both people who know crypto assets well and general users, and many of them have completed identity verification. At the same time, it invites users into the journey of improving and evolving the product. By connecting the token with the actual product experience, a project can not only create usefulness for its own token, but also involve users from an early stage of the development process, have them try the product's features and functions, check whether the product meets real human needs, and grow the product more effectively.

From the Pioneers' point of view, the Launchpad offers transparent, merit-based access to these tokens. Pioneers can choose to take part in different projects, access tokens through the release system, and use those tokens inside the products. This creates a cycle in which users engage with a product, evaluate its usefulness, and ask the project to provide useful services. Unlike Web2 products, users do not only use the products a company provides; they also become people who hold and use, from an early stage, the tokens the company issued.

Projects that release through Pi Launchpad are required to put the product first. In other words, a project must already have a working app, so that the token has usefulness immediately at the time of release.

The Launchpad system also supports the development of activity on the decentralized exchange (DEX, a system for trading without a central operator) within the Pi ecosystem. The Pi DEX currently exists on the testnet, but a healthy trading environment depends on legitimate tokens with real usefulness and liquidity being available. By letting product-first projects release ecosystem tokens, and by directing the Pi proceeds into liquidity pools with those tokens, Pi Launchpad is intended to help create a flow in which tokens with these features become available on the Pi DEX, and to meet the need for liquidity. This structure can help prevent early misuse on Mainnet, where the DEX and AMM (automated market maker, a system that sets prices and trades automatically) would overflow with speculative tokens that have no meaningful usefulness. With this method, tokens that fit the Pi ecosystem's usefulness-first policy can be created within the ecosystem, and it supports more active trading activity while meeting the goal of providing a safe place where Pioneers can take part.

For details of the original design thinking behind ecosystem tokens on Mainnet, readers are guided to read the Open Network anniversary article, watch the video by Pi founder Chengdiao Fan, and read the detailed design proposal on GitHub and give feedback.

With the Pi Day product release, in addition to reading materials and watching videos, people can now try the first version of Pi Launchpad and learn the system in actual operation. Note that during the test stage, the Launchpad product may be improved quickly and may change. For example, the length of each stage of a release, whether different steps are available, the information that can be used, and the calculation methods. The content is not necessarily the same on every visit. This speed of change and inconsistency will not happen in the Mainnet version. The fast improvement and change in the testnet version is intended as part of product testing, and its results are reflected in the design to reach the best version on Mainnet.

Note also that the overall ecosystem token design and the Pi Launchpad product may change based on community feedback on the design, the data collected during the testnet period, and feedback from projects that intend to issue such usefulness tokens.
