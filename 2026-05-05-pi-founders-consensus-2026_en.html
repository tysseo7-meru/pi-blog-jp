Pi Founders Dr. Chengdiao Fan and Dr. Nicolas Kokkalis Speak at Consensus 2026 on May 6-7

Published: May 5, 2026

Categories: general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

This week, Dr. Chengdiao Fan and Dr. Nicolas Kokkalis, the founders of Pi, will be introduced as speakers at Consensus 2026 (a large meeting about blockchain and digital money) in Miami. More than 20,000 people will take part at the venue.

On stage, the two will share their views on Pi's blockchain (a technology that keeps records of transactions in a shared form that is hard to change), identity verification, business models, and their way of thinking about practical use in the age of AI (artificial intelligence, computer systems that learn from data and make judgments).

The schedule is as follows.

  • Chengdiao Fan will speak on Wednesday, May 6, from 11:15 to 11:35 (EDT, Eastern Daylight Time in the United States) at the Convergence Stage.
  • Nicolas Kokkalis will speak in a panel discussion (a talk where several people discuss a topic together) on Thursday, May 7, from 10:15 to 10:45 (EDT) at the Convergence Stage.

Dr. Fan's talk, 'Aligning Web3, AI, and Blockchain for Utility' (bringing Web3 (the idea of a decentralized web based on blockchain), AI, and blockchain together for practical use), will explore how Pi's blockchain base, verified identity information, and network active around the world can support products that focus on practical use and business models for the AI age. This is especially relevant now. As AI greatly lowers the difficulty of making products, the center of competition is moving to real data, gaining users, and trusted human participation.

This talk presents a new view of crypto tokens (digital units of value issued on a blockchain) not as an exit for selling, but as a tool for long-lasting growth and spread in the real world.

Dr. Kokkalis's panel discussion, 'How to Prove You're Human in an AI World (Without Doxing Yourself)' (how to prove you are human in an AI world without revealing your personal information), will explore a situation where the system of trust on the internet is breaking down. This is because AI systems can now create bots (programs that act automatically) that make profiles and act like real users. Being a real person is the base for a meaningful connection with the real-world economy. For identity verification, Pi Network has built a KYC system (KYC, the process of checking a person's identity) that can be spread on a global scale and that meets the essential requirements needed for blockchain networks, marketplaces (places that connect sellers and buyers), and apps for the AI age.

The original article ends with a call to Pioneers (members of Pi) to meet at the venue.
