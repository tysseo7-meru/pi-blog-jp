Update to Pi App Studio Creation Pricing

Published: August 17, 2026

Categories: developer, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

From August 24, 2026, the way prices are set for creating and editing apps in Pi App Studio (a tool for making apps using AI) will change.

Until now, Pi has paid most of the cost, so creators only had to pay 0.25 Pi to create an app and 0.25 Pi to edit an app. However, the real cost of making apps with AI is considerably higher than that. To keep the low introductory price, Pi has been paying the difference.

After running it for a certain period, enough data has been collected about how creators use the product, and which apps are likely to gather real users and become useful. So the price will be adjusted to be closer to the real cost, while Pi's support continues. From now on, support will be focused only on apps that aim to be truly useful, and on creators who have shown a serious effort to make useful apps.

From August 24, there will be a standard price that more accurately reflects the real cost of the AI services used to generate an app. The standard price may vary depending on the amount of resources needed for creation or editing. This price includes no markup (extra amount added on top) at all on the cost of the original AI services.

Creators who have a sufficient number of real users for their own app will keep the supported price as before. Whether someone can receive the supported price is decided by whether they have made an app where there is real use by separate users other than the creator themselves, and utility is shown. This directs the network's resources to creators who focus on making apps that are actually used.

At the start of the new pricing, early eligibility conditions (rules for who qualifies) will be applied to existing data to decide who can keep the supported price. These conditions may change over time as App Studio develops, and as Pi learns more about creators and the apps being made.

Eligibility is not a one-time decision. Pi plans to review creators' eligibility regularly, based on newly produced data. That is, a creator who was not eligible for the supported price at first may become eligible later, if their app gathers more real users. The new pricing system also becomes an incentive (a reason to act) to make more useful and more attractive apps in order to receive the supported price. In the same way, the pricing system, eligibility conditions, and the number of creator tiers (levels or groups) may also change over time.

What existing creators can do before the change

The new pricing starts on August 24, 2026. In the period before that, existing creators can be encouraged to improve their apps and have more people try them.

The following are listed as things creators should work on.

  • Improve the usefulness, quality, and ease of use of existing apps
  • Share the app with peers and other Pioneers (Pi users) who might find it useful
  • Collect opinions from real users, and improve based on those opinions
  • Keep making apps that solve real problems, or apps that provide value to the Pi community

To repeat, creators who could not gain eligibility at the time the new pricing starts will continue to have chances to gain eligibility in later regular reviews.

Why Pi is making this change

Pi App Studio is a programming tool that uses generative AI (AI that creates content from instructions such as text), so that people can easily make and publish Pi apps whether they are technical or not. A guided app-creation workflow (the flow for making an app along a guide) is provided, and it helps creators turn their expert knowledge and ideas into working products that can be published directly to the Pi ecosystem (the whole set of related apps and services).

At first, by giving a large subsidy (paying part of the cost) for all creation and editing, App Studio was made easy for many people to try, and the platform (the base system) was able to observe and evaluate how apps are created.

However, not every app made in App Studio grows into a product that other people use. If all creation continues to be supported in the same way, it becomes a considerable waste of resources. This includes funding many apps whose use does not spread, such as apps made only for tests, experiments, and spam (unwanted mass creation).

The new approach is to use these resources more efficiently and effectively. By directing larger support to creators of apps that are actually useful and gather real users, Pi can focus resources on creators who produce apps that help the ecosystem. At the same time, the new pricing gives all creators a clear motive to improve their apps, spread them, and make products that people really want to use.
