Pi KYC Validator Rewards: First Round of Distributions Completed

Published: April 3, 2026

Categories: pi-day, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

The first round of distribution of KYC validator rewards is complete. The first round started on Pi Day 2026. Every KYC validator with a Mainnet wallet received the reward on the Mainnet blockchain.

More than 1 million human validators successfully completed more than 526 million validations. Combined with the AI processing in the system, this work led to 18 million people being identity-verified around the world. The completed KYC validation work showed that Pi's human workforce, spread across the world, has the power, consistency, and scale to produce meaningful real-world results, especially in areas that need human judgment and input.

Now that the first distribution is done, it is a good time to start contributing. Pioneers are encouraged to become KYC validators, complete accurate validation work, and join Pi's distributed workforce, showing the power of distributed human input at a global scale. This is especially relevant for Pioneers as Pi continues to develop human-in-the-loop (a setup where humans take part in an AI process) and its KYC-as-a-service system.

By contributing to KYC validation, Pioneers can benefit from future rounds of KYC validator reward distributions and from other distributed work opportunities. For example, in this first distribution, the price per validation was 22 times the current base mining rate.

Pi is now working to improve the calculation method used to evaluate validators' performance, ahead of the second round of distribution. A video explanation of the reward calculation has also been released.

The rewards were sent to validators' Mainnet Pi wallets. To receive a payment, a KYC validator needs to have a Mainnet Pi wallet. It can be obtained by completing the Mainnet Checklist and migrating to the Mainnet. Or, eligible Pioneers can create a Mainnet-ready wallet (such as a Fast Track Wallet) directly in the wallet app and confirm that wallet in the Mainnet Checklist.

How the rewards are calculated (excerpt from the Pi Day 2026 announcement)

The following is an excerpt that was originally part of the Pi Day 2026 announcement. It gives the details of the reward calculation, the reasoning behind it, and insight into what Pi's large, distributed workforce means for AI.

It restates the original design from when the KYC app started several years ago. When each Pioneer migrates to the Mainnet, they pay 1 Pi into the pool (the fund) that pays rewards to the human KYC validators. KYC validators' work should be paid from this pool as long as it contributes to the accuracy of application processing, whether the final KYC result of the application is a pass or a fail. This is because correctly judging a failure is also a contribution to the network. However, people whose KYC application fails do not migrate to the Mainnet, so they cannot pay the 1 Pi.

For that reason, Pi's validator rewards pool is shared equally among all eligible work done in that period. Dividing the number of Pi in the pool by the total number of successful validations sets the price per validation. A KYC validator's reward is this price multiplied by the number of validations that validator completed successfully. The first round of rewards was calculated by taking a snapshot (a record at one point in time) of the pool and the total amount of successful work before the distribution. That point in time was up to March 5, 2026. New Pi entering the pool and newly successful validations will be handled in the second and later rounds of distribution.

Following the method above, at the time of the snapshot, the validator rewards pool held 16,568,774 Pi. This is because 16,568,774 Pioneers had migrated to the Mainnet. The number of successful human validations was 526,970,631, and the human validators who did them numbered 1,094,680.

The period covered by the first round of validator rewards is a time when the KYC validator network was still in its launch stage. Many of the validations in that period were used to train human validators so that they could do later validation work skillfully and accurately. This early stage also contributed to forming the KYC system itself, including building and scaling the human validator workforce. For these reasons, the Pi Foundation decided to contribute 10 million Pi to the pool to supplement the first distribution.

As a result, the final calculation of the price per validation in the first round is as follows. (16,568,774 Pi + 10 million Pi) ÷ 526,970,631 successful validations = 0.0504179 Pi per validation. This price per validation is equal to 21 times the current base mining rate.

To receive a reward in this first round, a validator needed to have completed, by March 5, 2026, at least 50 validations that reached a majority agreement. Payments are sent automatically to the validator's Mainnet Pi wallet, so validators need to have a Mainnet wallet ready and confirmed in the Mainnet Checklist. A detailed breakdown of validator rewards and performance status can be checked in the validator dashboard inside the KYC app.

Each Pioneer's KYC application has needed an average of 20 validations before a final decision. This is because the KYC system is designed to protect privacy: one application is split into multiple different validation tasks, and each task needs two validations by at least two different validators. The tasks include liveness checks (checks that the person is present in person), document checks, photo matching, data matching, name checks, and so on. Applications with special requests, such as a name update, or applications that need extra checks, such as multiple liveness checks or resubmissions, may need more validations.

The unit price of the rewards may change from round to round, depending on factors such as the number of Pioneers who migrated and the total pool of validated reviews in that period. Also, as AI processing in KYC increases and validations meant to train the early workforce decrease, fewer human validations will be needed per application. The total number of validations will therefore fall, and the pool will be shared among fewer validations, so the price per validation may rise in future rounds, by the calculation. Future distributions may also use additional or different criteria based on a validator's consistency and accuracy.

What the distributed human workforce showed

Apart from the distribution of KYC validator rewards, the data above shows several large achievements of the Pi community. Not only did more than 16.5 million people complete KYC through this KYC system and migrate to the Mainnet; the scale, consistency, and coordination of the Pi community's large, distributed human workforce showed that it can produce meaningful real-world results at a global scale, in work that needs human judgment and input. This does not only honor the validators' efforts in the KYC process. It also shows the potential for the Pi community to contribute to other kinds of work where humans are needed, for the network and the future AI economy, such as the many human-in-the-loop processes in AI, and the human input needed to train and improve AI abilities.

Many AI projects talk about the importance of humans and human input in such systems, and have built the base that makes human input possible, but they face a big challenge: few or no actual humans take part. Pi solved the hard problem first. Pi already has a large, active network, and it has shown the will and ability to contribute. In addition, Pi has its own global payment system through a blockchain, which can handle paying the workforce at a large scale. For example, in just one app, 526,970,631 tasks were completed by 1,094,680 people, and their rewards were paid in Pi through the Pi blockchain. The KYC validator workforce and its results have built the foundation for future human-in-the-loop products, both at the base level and through third-party apps. This widens the ways humans can contribute to creating and improving AI and the future economy. Details about these wider meanings are planned to be shared in a later announcement.
