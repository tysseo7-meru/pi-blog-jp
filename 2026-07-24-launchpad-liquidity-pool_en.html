Pi Launchpad Distributes SLICE Test Tokens and Introduces Liquidity Pool Price Tracking

Published: July 24, 2026

Categories: general, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Launchpad has completed the distribution of SLICE, its second Testnet token.

The purpose of releasing test tokens is to introduce Pioneers to the new ecosystem token system, and to let the community learn how to take part in decentralized finance (DeFi, financial services carried out on a blockchain without going through banks and the like). By handling test tokens, Pioneers can gain practical financial knowledge about liquidity pools (a setup that holds two kinds of assets ready for exchange), swaps (exchanging one asset for another), and automatic token pricing.

Testing is also a chance for Pi to collect valuable data and Pioneer feedback. This helps improve and refine the design and mechanics of Launchpad for the Mainnet version.

From June 11 to 28, more than 242,000 participating Pioneers committed a total of 15.92 million Test-Pi to receive 10 million SLICE test tokens.

Unlike the first test token release, SLICE is connected to Slice of Pi, a real, working third-party game. So the engagement bonus feature (a participation bonus) can be tested in a real app rather than a dummy (pretend) app, and Pi can gain information that helps the Launchpad design before the Mainnet version. This helps align the purpose of Pi's ecosystem tokens, which put the product first: they are mainly not for raising funds, but for supporting real use, user acquisition, and participation.

The SLICE release introduced a simpler participation flow, centered on the amount of Test-Pi that Pioneers choose to commit. Launchpad automatically calculates the related hold kept back for fair access (the fair access hold), and shows the participation benefit as a percentage bonus on the SLICE received.

The distribution is complete, and Pioneers can now explore the experience after the release. The Launchpad screen shows each person's allocation details, the release price and the effective token price, access to the SLICE liquidity pool, and a chart that tracks changes in the price of SLICE against Test-Pi.

How the SLICE liquidity pool works

In the Pi Launchpad system, a project issues a token as a tool to gain users for its own application, and builds that token directly into product features such as access, payments, rewards, and governance (taking part in how the project is run). The Pi proceeds from a token release do not go to the issuing project. Instead, they enter a liquidity pool together with the ecosystem token. This creates a base of healthy liquidity (ease of exchange) from the start.

In the SLICE test release, the result is a liquidity pool holding SLICE and Test-Pi. In addition to matching individual buyers and sellers through the traditional decentralized order book (a list of buy and sell orders) on the Pi DEX (decentralized exchange), this pool uses the calculation method of an automated market maker (a system that sets prices and exchanges assets automatically), which calculates swaps based on the amounts of both assets the pool currently holds.

When a Pioneer obtains SLICE using Test-Pi, Test-Pi is added to the pool and SLICE is taken out of the pool. Because the pool then holds more Test-Pi and less SLICE, the displayed price of the remaining SLICE goes up. When a Pioneer exchanges SLICE for Test-Pi, the opposite happens. SLICE is added to the pool and Test-Pi is taken out, so the displayed price of SLICE may go down.

These swaps are calculated using the constant product formula (a formula that keeps the result of multiplying the two amounts the same). In simple terms, before fees are considered, the pool adjusts the amounts of both assets so that the product of the two reserve amounts stays constant during a swap.

A simple example of a liquidity pool

Imagine a pool that starts with 10 SLICE and 10 Test-Pi. Under the constant product formula, it works as follows.

SLICE reserve × Test-Pi reserve = a constant product. So at the start, 10 SLICE × 10 Test-Pi = 100.

When a Pioneer takes out 5 SLICE, 5 SLICE remain in the pool. To keep the product at 100, 5 SLICE × the new Test-Pi reserve = 100. So the new Test-Pi reserve is 100 ÷ 5 = 20 Test-Pi.

Because the pool started with 10 Test-Pi, ignoring fees, the Pioneer needs to add 10 Test-Pi. After that, the pool holds 20 Test-Pi and 5 SLICE. With this new balance, the displayed price of SLICE becomes higher. This example shows that the demand expressed through swaps directly changes the pool's balance and the resulting token price.

Pioneers can follow this movement through the new price chart feature, and explore how the token price reacts as swaps happen on the Testnet.

Explore SLICE in the Pi Launchpad app

SLICE exists only on the Testnet and will never move to the Mainnet. Still, Pioneers can use SLICE to learn how the Launchpad system, liquidity pools, swaps, and token pricing work. Taking part also helps Pi collect data and community feedback to improve the product before Mainnet token releases.

Today, you can open Pi Launchpad in the Pi Browser and explore SLICE.
