Pi Launchpad Releases Improved Participation Flow and Updated Model, Along with Second Test Token on Testnet

Published: June 11, 2026

Categories: announcement, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Launchpad has updated its participation flow and model. The update takes in data and feedback from IRRA, the first Testnet token released on Pi Day 2026. Launchpad is Pi's system for apps to issue tokens.

The purpose of the test token releases is to introduce Pioneers (Pi users) to the new ecosystem token system and let the community learn how to take part in DeFi (decentralized finance, financial services carried out on a blockchain), while collecting useful data and Pioneer feedback to improve and refine the design and mechanics of Launchpad for the Mainnet version.

In the first Launchpad test token, more than 478,000 participating Pioneers staked (set aside) a total of 36.05 million Test-Pi, and more than 198,000 Pioneers subscribed 14.72 million Test-Pi. Of the subscribed Test-Pi, 11.71 million was applied to obtaining 10 million IRRA tokens.

These results provided useful data and showed areas where the Launchpad experience needs improvement. Some of the findings are as follows.

  • Some Pioneers found it hard to understand the relationship between staking and subscribing, and how each one affected them.
  • The order of the actions — staking, then subscribing — caused drop-offs. Some users staked Test-Pi but did not go on to subscribe, or did not subscribe enough against their own stake, so they could not actually take part in the token acquisition process.
  • The first release had too many moving parts and too much complexity around staking, subscribing, and how the two interact. This made it hard for some users to decide how to allocate the amount they wanted to participate with.

The second Testnet token, SLICE, was released to test these improvements. The improvements include updates to the interface, the user experience, and the Launchpad mechanics. Also, unlike the first test, SLICE is connected to Slice of Pi, a real working app rather than a demo, so Pi can collect real usage data and use it to inform the Launchpad design before the Mainnet version. SLICE is a Testnet token and will never move to the Mainnet.

A simpler and clearer participation flow

In the first test token release, staking and subscribing were separate steps on separate screens. Pioneers had to enter the stake amount by hand, then enter the subscription amount separately, and at the same time understand how the two actions affected their participation.

To improve this, participation in the second test token centers on the subscription amount, which directly affects token acquisition. First, the Pioneer chooses the amount of Test-Pi they want to subscribe, either by picking from quick options or entering a custom amount. Next, Launchpad automatically calculates the related “fair access hold” (Pi set aside temporarily for fair participation; it was previously called a stake or PiPower) based on a fixed formula, and shows the subscription amount, the hold amount, and the total together before confirmation. After that, both transactions are submitted in order from a single screen.

The fair access hold is designed to support a wider distribution of tokens and to reduce the chance that large holders crowd out participants with small subscriptions. The required hold grows in stages as the subscription amount the Pioneer chooses grows. A larger subscription requires a proportionally larger hold, which keeps access open for people who want to participate with a small amount. The word “hold” also expresses the temporary nature of this system. The fair access hold is not a purchase of the issuing project's token. The Pi set aside remains the participating Pioneer's Pi, and it is returned after the hold period.

The Launchpad model was also updated. The ratio between the hold and the subscription now follows a fixed curve, instead of varying fully based on participants' behavior. This is meant to reduce the number of moving parts, make the interaction between the hold and the subscription simpler, and keep the fair participation effect that the hold is meant to have.

Other updates include, for clarity, showing the benefit of participation as a percentage bonus on the tokens the Pioneer receives, rather than as a discount on the token price. The “View details” also became a single scrollable screen, instead of a central screen with three separate action pages.

Together, these changes are meant to make Launchpad easier to understand, especially for Pioneers learning about such systems for the first time. A second test token release is therefore needed to test these new updates and evaluate whether they meet the goal. Pioneers were welcomed to share feedback on the second test token release. The original article showed where to submit feedback.

How to take part in the second test token release

Pioneers were encouraged to try the SLICE test token in Pi Launchpad, use the Slice of Pi app, check the updated participation flow, and share feedback that helps improve the Launchpad system.

SLICE is connected to Slice of Pi. Slice of Pi is a third-party game integrated with Pi, which shows Pi game use cases through Pi payments and the Pi Ad Network (Pi's advertising system). This connection lets Pi Launchpad test usage input from a working app, and helps it understand how product use can affect participation in a token release. Pioneers can use a real product, understand its use case, and take part in a test release tied to that product experience. This strengthens the product-first purpose of ecosystem tokens.

Participation in the SLICE test token release is open until Pi2Day, June 28, 2026.

How to participate is as follows.

  • Open the Pi Browser and go to the Pi Launchpad app.
  • Check the test token and the issuing project.
  • Choose a subscription amount in Test-Pi. Launchpad automatically calculates the related fair access hold, and shows the subscription amount, the hold amount, and the total before confirmation.
  • After checking the details, complete the payment flow to confirm your participation.
  • Use the Slice of Pi app. Using it helps you judge whether the product is useful and worth taking part in its token release. It also helps the project collect user feedback and confirm its use case. Depending on how much you use it, you may qualify for a percentage bonus on the SLICE tokens you receive.

As with the first test token, SLICE tokens exist only on the Testnet and will never move to the Mainnet. They are a way for Pioneers to learn the Launchpad system in a safe environment, while Pi collects data and community feedback to improve the Launchpad product before real Mainnet token releases.

A recap: what are Pi ecosystem tokens?

The ecosystem tokens released on Pi Launchpad are designed mainly not for raising funds, but for user acquisition and product use. In this model, a project issues a token as a tool to gain users for its own app, and builds the token directly into product features — for example, enabling access, payments, rewards, and participation in how the project is run inside the app. The Pi proceeds from a token release do not go to the issuing project. Instead, they enter a liquidity pool (a collection of funds that makes trading go smoothly) together with the ecosystem token, creating a base of healthy liquidity from the start.

For projects, ecosystem tokens become a system for gathering real users, collecting feedback, and testing and improving the product. Companies can show innovative products to Pi's large global user base, which includes both users who know crypto well and general users, many of whom are identity-verified.

From the Pioneer's point of view, Pi Launchpad offers transparent, merit-based access to these tokens. Pioneers can choose to take part in different projects, access their tokens through the release system, and then use those tokens inside the product itself.

The Launchpad system also supports the growth of DEX (decentralized exchange) activity within the Pi ecosystem. The Pi DEX currently exists on the Testnet, but a healthy trading environment depends on legitimate tokens with real use and liquidity being available.
