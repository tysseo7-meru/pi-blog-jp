Open Network Anniversary Video With Pi Founders Chengdiao Fan and Nicolas Kokkalis

Published: February 20, 2026

Categories: general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

To celebrate the first anniversary of Open Network, Pi founders Nicolas Kokkalis and Chengdiao Fan answered a series of questions. The aim is to give Pioneers (Pi users) a detailed view of Pi's plan, way of working, and current efforts since Open Network. It also shows more clearly the direction and priorities that will guide future development. In the full video, these topics are covered in depth. What follows is the transcript.

Transcript: Introduction

Nicolas Kokkalis said, Pioneers, I am Dr. Nicolas Kokkalis. Chengdiao Fan introduced herself by saying, I am Dr. Chengdiao Fan. The two of them expressed joy at celebrating the first anniversary of the launch of Pi's Open Network together with the community.

Chengdiao Fan said that Open Network, which officially started on February 20, 2025, was a turning point in Pi's history. It connected Pi's blockchain, its community of people whose identity has been checked, and its Web3 ecosystem (an ecosystem for a decentralized web that uses blockchain) with the outside world. As many Pioneers know, the Pi community worked together for 6 years, with a great deal of work and contribution, to build the ecosystem. This was to make sure Pi was ready in the Open Network stage and that its real use could last.

Nicolas Kokkalis said that since Open Network started, Pi's path has kept moving forward in line with its long-term view and plan. That goal is to create a cryptocurrency that anyone can access widely, that is open, that is based on real use, and that is widely spread. This way of working — focusing on real use, large-scale spread, and ease of use — is needed for any blockchain to succeed. It is also what makes Pi different from the blockchain industry as a whole.

In the past year alone, there was big progress in Pi's ecosystem activity, in app development efforts and tools, and in the release of new products and real uses across the whole platform. Main examples include the AI-assisted Pi App Studio tools, the Pi Network Ventures effort that invests in new startups and businesses, and features such as DEX and AMM and token creation.

Chengdiao Fan said that in the coming year, Pi will focus on several things. It will grow chances to create apps and real uses. It will combine Web3 tools with the creation of new ideas. It will solve basic problems, such as digital identity information, for bringing cryptocurrency into the real world. It will also work on interoperability (the ability of different systems to work together), better ease of use, and more meaningful participation across the whole ecosystem. While growing the real use of Pi, Pi is improving the tools and experiences that make it easy for developers, businesses, and Pioneers to build, take part, and create. She said this is how Pi will make its long-term view real and keep fulfilling the true promise of blockchain and Web3. She then said the two of them would answer a series of questions to look more deeply at Pi's plan and way of working since Open Network and in the future.

Why Does Pi Feel Different from Other Blockchains? Why Is Real Use Important?

Chengdiao Fan said she feels Pi is different from other blockchains because Pi really is different. When Open Network started 1 year ago, she described Pi as something that does not fit the usual pattern, and that is still true now. Pi sets itself apart in several basic ways. Pi has never held an ICO (an initial sale of tokens). Mining (the action of earning Pi in the app) is free, for ease of access. Pi is mobile-first. Pi's Mainnet (the live blockchain) is fully KYC-passed (everyone has passed an identity check). There are tens of millions of identity-checked users around the world. And Pi has stressed real use from the start.

Across the whole cryptocurrency industry, there are thousands of cryptocurrencies and digital assets. In that world, trading naturally becomes a large focus. The systems of trading themselves — DeFi (decentralized finance), liquidity (how easily something can be traded), and market base systems — are important areas of new ideas. But beyond how trading is done, there is a more basic question. What are people actually trading?

The problem Pi tried to solve is not only whether trading is possible, or how well it can be done. It is the much harder problem of what should be traded. If the only goal were to make trading possible, a large number of fake or meaningless tokens could be issued, of the kind seen even on a testnet. Trading could start right away. As long as people took part, trading volume could be high. But she asked: if those tokens do not stand for something real, what meaning is there?

The hard problem is to work out how a token can be used in real-world apps, in commerce that includes real goods and services, or to take part in a meaningful way in the processes of business and production. That is where real use comes in.

In the past, Pi has stressed using Pi for real goods and services. This includes local commerce and Pi apps across the Pi Browser, such as e-commerce, game, social, and education apps. Utility tokens — whether Pi itself or ecosystem tokens built on Pi — need to be built into specific products and real flows of use. They must not exist only inside abstract financial systems.

Real use is also connected to the identity check through KYC that Pi focuses on. Pi's fully KYC-passed network became the base for matching blockchain with real-world assets and systems of production.

She said she is sometimes asked why apps that won Pi hackathons look very different from typical cryptocurrency projects. They are not pure DeFi products. They look like dating apps or e-commerce platforms (online systems for buying and selling). That difference is on purpose. Pi puts first apps that solve real-world problems and meet real-world needs. They are not creations that exist only inside financial systems. DeFi systems become important only when real use and production are involved. Making fast, large-volume trading possible is easy. Making tokens usable inside real products that support real new ideas and economic activity is much harder.

But choosing that hard path is worth it. She said they believe Web3 can reach a meaningful breakthrough only when people patiently try to solve this complex and hard problem: how blockchain and cryptocurrency systems can be used to really help real-world production. This path needs more time and patience, and it may look different. But this is the path Pi has chosen since day one. It is also the path that the whole cryptocurrency industry will in the end need to follow to overcome its own problems.

This way of thinking goes back to Pi's original design. Pi was designed so anyone could access it freely. Anyone could mine with no money barrier and no cost of their own. That design made wide spread and open participation possible. It also made it possible for the network and all participants to work patiently on the hard work of building a well-working ecosystem based on real use. Many cryptocurrencies are already going down other paths. She invited people to try this path together with Pi, for free.

What Is the Network Working on Now?

Nicolas Kokkalis said the network will of course keep doing the general work that is important for Pioneers, such as KYC and migration. It will also keep working on tools that let developers build on Pi more easily, and on different efforts to increase real use on Pi.

KYC and migration remain the top priority. Pi is raising its KYC processing power and removing blocks for more users. It is putting more AI into the KYC process to raise speed, and it is continuing migration. It will also make second migrations possible, so more Pioneers can take part fully in the Mainnet ecosystem. Rewards for KYC validators (people who review identity checks) are also on track to be rolled out within this quarter, in a safe way that can grow.

For developer tools and support, Pi keeps supporting developers so they can start and grow real use. It does this by lowering the barrier to building on Pi, through better tools and easier connections. This includes new tools such as a much faster Pi payment setup.

AI and the creation of real use are also important. Pi is growing App Studio with wider access, new payment connections, and deeper AI features. This helps apps move from an idea to real use faster.

At the network level, Pi keeps working on nodes (computers that support the network), protocol upgrades (updates to the basic rules of the system), and parts such as DEX features and liquidity pools. All of this is to support real, easy-to-use apps and activity on Pi.

Pi Network Ventures keeps focusing on moving forward real-world use and production on Pi Network. It keeps investing in startups and businesses, no matter their stage or industry.

In the end, he said Pi Network will keep working to support and grow a strong community. It will do this through events such as Pi Day, hackathons, and local commerce. It will also do this through ongoing education and sharing about Pi's way of working, products, features, turning points, and ecosystem.

What Are Pi Ecosystem Tokens? What Is the Design Idea for Ecosystem Tokens on Mainnet?

Chengdiao Fan said Pi ecosystem tokens are tokens created by the community and issued on Pi. Ecosystem tokens have already been released on the testnet, and they are in the final stage of being put in place on Mainnet. Technology and products clearly matter. But she thinks the most important part on Mainnet will be their design.

As she also touched on in a recent talk at Token2049, being able to issue tokens is an important and strong power of Web3. But a central problem across the whole cryptocurrency field is the gap between token design and real new ideas. Tokens on most other cryptocurrency networks work mainly as tools to raise funds. But even with this way of working, most of those projects often cannot provide meaningful real use and new ideas. She sees this as a structural problem.

So what is the solution? From a business point of view, what else can a token really be used for? If startup and business founders cannot use tokens mainly to raise funds, a question comes up. When you think of the work needed to run one, why issue and run a token at all? Here, Pi is in a good position to show a different answer.

Pi's way is to build cryptocurrency tokens directly into the process of developing products and new ideas. Pi strongly encourages and stresses a focus on real use. This is because real use makes long-term stability, lasting success, and success itself possible for any blockchain project. Pi itself is a utility token. Ecosystem tokens on Pi are also encouraged to focus on real use and to be used for real use in the same way. Many kinds of tokens can be issued on a blockchain. But Pi's current launch program (a system that supports launches) intentionally encourages a completely new design system based on Pi's existing resources and base. That is, utility tokens designed to gain users.

A token for gaining users through Pi's launch program means the following. A project issues a token to meet its need to gain users for its own product. It builds these tokens into the product for uses based on real use. Users can access these tokens through the launch program. After that, they can use the tokens inside the project's product. Pi is staked (deposited and locked for a purpose) and used to adjust and smoothly run the system described above.

Pi has millions of users around the world. They include not only users who know a lot about cryptocurrency. More importantly, they also include ordinary users, many of whom have already had their identity checked. For people who make products and for businesses, high-quality users and reach around the world are some of the most valuable resources. They are needed for a product to succeed.

Pi's goal is to design ecosystem tokens as a strong tool. With them, startups and businesses can gain users, with the users' agreement, from places including Pi Network, and can help their business grow. Gaining users in the early stage is usually very costly for a startup. By using a Web3 tool such as Pi ecosystem tokens, a startup can lower the cost of gaining users. At the same time, it can involve users directly in its project, get their opinions, test product features and functions, and make sure the product is used to meet real human needs.

Users who are gained can also help hold a business responsible for the service it provides. If a product cannot keep its real use, quality, and involvement, users may leave the product. Natural gaining and keeping of users is a natural system that pushes projects to build and provide useful services. It fits the original aim of real products with real new ideas.

On the other hand, from the user's point of view, the free choice to take part in and use different products is still there, as it has always been in Web2. The difference is this. Users can access utility tokens issued by projects through Pi's launch program, DEX, and AMM. They can then use these tokens inside the projects' products.

In this way, she thinks this design can better match Web3's special ability — issuing tokens — with support for new products. Pi ecosystem tokens do not copy the form of existing tokens. In fact, Pi has intentionally tried to avoid the usual way of working. This is because many problems in Web3 come from how tokens have usually been designed. This design will also evolve as it is repeated in practice.

Another equally important difference is in the kinds of projects that are encouraged to start on Pi. Pi does not want to focus mainly on token issuers who issue a token first and then try to build a business around it. Instead, Pi wants to put first businesses that have real products and that start from a real problem to solve. On top of that, they can use a token as a tool to do things better and to grow the product.

Of course, ecosystem tokens also involve technical points, such as interoperability and standard cryptocurrency connections. Pi uses the efforts and new ideas of the whole cryptocurrency field.

Pi's focus is not on chasing tokens for their own sake, or systems and connections on their own. Its focus is on making useful tokens based on real use possible, and on supporting the real new ideas behind them. From there, she said, Pi's platform will design tools, make connections possible, and support the base these products need to really succeed and grow.

What Does Pi KYC Mean? What Comes Next?

Nicolas Kokkalis said that, as Pioneers know, the community has built Pi's KYC solution together over many years. This goes from the base technology and product design to starting a network of human validators (reviewers) around the world. It includes building the flow of validator work across many regions of the world, and creating a design that protects user privacy. Because Pioneers are spread around the world, the Pi KYC solution had to reach wide coverage by place and the ability to grow. Beyond standard identity checks, the Pi KYC solution also builds sanctions matching (checking against sanctions lists) and law-following checks into a single system.

There are several reasons why Pi chose to invest so heavily in KYC. From Pi Network's point of view, it is the base of the network's completeness and realness. Pi also wanted to lower the need for Pioneers to pay out of their own pocket for identity checks, and to make sure the whole community can access it. From the point of view of web use and the industry, Pi sees KYC as an important unsolved problem in Web3. It is a needed step before blockchain technology spreads on a large scale. These points were the driving force behind building KYC in-house, instead of leaving it to an outside service.

But KYC is not only something Pi needs inside itself. The next stage of this work is to treat KYC itself as a service. That is, KYC as a platform ability.

Identity information is the base for meaningful connection with the real-world economy. Every transfer of value, ownership, and change of ownership always raises the question of who the participants are. That is, at its base, a question of identity information, and therefore a question of KYC.

In the near future, as a growth of what Pi has already developed, Pi will provide its KYC technology and products to other projects as a service, not as data. This is important. Those projects may be in Web3 or may be ordinary businesses. This means providing an API (a way for systems to connect to each other) that lets outside services use the Pi KYC solution on a large scale. KYC providers already exist, but Pi's way has special points in several ways. They are coverage across places around the world, the ability to grow, a mixed form that combines AI and human checking, and the completeness of the solution. Pi KYC handles not only identity checks. It also handles sanctions checks for following the law, the flow of human validator work, comparisons across networks, and different forms of rules and regulations. It also includes extra methods such as palm authentication (authentication using the palm print). These strengthen matching and authentication further, and meet industry standards for identity checks.

Looking further ahead, the fact that Pi Network itself is KYC-passed becomes a strong shared resource for the whole network. Users keep managing their own identity information and data themselves. But they also gain the choice to take part in apps built on a network of people whose identity has been checked. That network provides real use to outside services that need trusted human identity checks.

This matters far beyond Pi. Solving KYC is a condition that must be met first for many major Web3 problems, including real-world assets. Looking more widely, in an AI-led world full of bots and agents that can copy human behavior more and more, being able to answer the simple but important question of who is real becomes essential.

So far, the KYC of tens of millions of Pioneers has already been completed. By opening KYC as a service to other networks and apps, he said, it will also become an important entrance for users outside Pi to join the Pi ecosystem.

Why Does Pi Focus on AI? What Does AI Have to Do with Blockchain?

Chengdiao Fan said AI is changing, at a basic level, how value and production are created in the world. This change will certainly bring new problems for humanity. As AI takes on more and more production, traditional labor is no longer a reliable base for how wealth is shared out. An important question comes up here. How can we make sure that the value AI creates is shared in a way that really improves the quality of life for everyone? It must not become even more concentrated among people who already control capital.

This is a central problem Pi is trying to deal with. Pi sees the ultimate real use of blockchain technology as a system for social sharing and re-sharing that can be checked and can grow. Over the past few years, Pi has built a widely spread network as the base for that goal. But having a spread network alone is not enough. To reach that ultimate real use, both existing production and new production need to be brought on-chain (onto the blockchain).

There are big problems in doing that. For existing forms of production, one of the biggest barriers is identity information. That means identity-checked identity information and identity-checked ownership. These are conditions that must be met before large-scale real-world production can move on-chain. Pi Network has invested heavily in solving this problem. It did so through a KYC solution that makes identity checks possible on a world scale and that provides the base for trusted participation in on-chain economic activity.

For new production, the situation is even clearer. The next generation of apps will almost certainly use AI, or will be built on the idea of AI. But the AI app layer is still at an early stage. Today, most user interaction with AI stays centered on chatbots. But the AI industry is actively testing richer apps, such as new forms of social networks, productivity tools (tools that help people work), and AI-assisted flows of work.

For that reason, in the short and medium term, Pi's focus where AI and blockchain meet is the AI app layer. Pi App Studio uses AI to help create apps. Simpler libraries and tools for developers are meant to let third-party AI developers build on Pi easily. At the plan level, Pi has combined a bottom-up way (building up from below) and a top-down way (leading from above). Bottom-up efforts such as App Studio can have a high ratio of signal to noise (useful results compared with useless ones). But they make it possible to create a large number of apps. That is expected and acceptable. At the same time, Pi balances this with a more top-down plan with a stronger signal, through Pi Network Ventures. Pi Network Ventures invests each year in carefully reviewed teams and works with them in a closer way.

Between these two plans, there are middle efforts such as hackathons and developer programs. They combine grass-roots efforts with more organized guidance. Together, these plans have tried to start, grow, and expand a lively ecosystem of AI-using apps on Pi.

Looking further ahead, Pi's involvement with AI also reaches the AI base itself. This includes distributed computing (sharing processing across many computers) for AI training, inference (using a trained AI to give answers), and reinforcement learning (learning by trial and reward). Pi is in a good position for this field. It already has a large network of computer nodes (over 350,000). It also has a world network of identity-checked human participants. They can choose to provide real human input for AI training, labeling (adding correct answers to data), and reinforcement learning, in exchange for payment in cryptocurrency from third-party clients.

Distributed AI training is still at the research stage in the world. Distributed AI training matches naturally with a spread network. It can also help deal with some of the limits of centralized training, such as data center limits, energy concentration, catastrophic forgetting (the problem of suddenly losing what was learned), and bottlenecks in the overall state. Big problems remain. But these are exactly the kinds of problems that need lasting research and long-term effort. Pi is actively exploring why and how a shift from fully centralized AI training to a more distributed way can be possible and efficient. Turning this into a meaningful contribution to the AI base needs much deeper research.

Human participation is also an important part. Pi's network of identity-checked humans provides a special resource for human input that can grow and is real, inside AI systems. Payment for it is handled directly through blockchain-based payments. Together, these parts — distributed computing, identity-checked human input, and blockchain-based adjustment — provide a new way toward AI training and its base.

Trying to solve the world's hard problems is a long-term effort for anyone in the world. It means starting early is even more important. This is because anything that can truly be defended, whether technology or a network effect, takes time to build. For example, what Pi Network has reached, and what gives it a plan advantage and protection, did not appear overnight. These include Pi's KYC system, its scale, its network of millions of identity-checked humans, its hundreds of thousands of computer nodes, and the attention and involvement of a community of tens of millions of people. Instead, they took years to build, with the effort of the whole Pi community. In the same way, contributing in a meaningful way to the AI base through distributed systems is extremely hard. She said that is exactly why it needs to be explored early, patiently, and over a long period.
