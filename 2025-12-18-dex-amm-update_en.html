Pi DEX and AMM Liquidity Pools Update, Plus Community Holiday Commerce Event!

Published: December 18, 2025

Categories: update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

The features of the Pi DEX (decentralized exchange — an exchange with no central operator) and the AMM (automated market maker — a system that sets prices and makes exchanges automatically) liquidity pools (funds put together so that people can trade) have been updated to make them easier to use and understand. These blockchain features were first released on the Pi Testnet (the test network) for testing purposes. They give developers and Pioneers a place to safely try out how decentralized finance (DeFi — financial services without intermediaries) works on Pi.

Changes were made based on the first use, testing, and feedback from users. The aim is to keep tokens on Pi tied to building real uses, to increase the real use of Pi, and to make the DEX and AMM experience easier to use and more transparent. The updates include improvements to the interface, organizing liquidity more effectively around pairs priced in Pi, extra signals that help users judge whether a token can be trusted, and a system that ranks tokens by liquidity.

Cleanup and interface improvements

The cleanup and interface updates to the DEX and AMM features improved the overall feel of using them. As users keep testing these DeFi features, moving around the screens and understanding how to use them has become easier.

By making the features easier to use and reducing the effort needed, these design updates better support the original purpose of the DEX and AMM on Testnet. That purpose is to let Pioneers get used to decentralized finance tools and features, to improve Pioneers' financial knowledge, and to let Pioneers and developers practice decentralized trading and liquidity in a safe way using test Pi.

A new explainer video shows the updated interface and how these DeFi tools work.

Pi-centered pairs and better organization of liquidity

Liquidity on the Pi Testnet DEX is now organized around pairs priced in Pi, so Pi became the common base for trading across the whole DEX. For a Pi-centered DEX, choosing Pi, the asset with the most liquidity, as the base follows good practice seen in similar systems.

By gathering liquidity around Pi as a common base, existing liquidity is organized into fewer, more active pools instead of being spread across many thin pools. This does not create new liquidity, but as participation and trading volume grow, it makes the available liquidity work better for trading.

For example, if token A and token B each have a trading pair priced in Pi, the prices of both A and B are set in relation to the price of Pi. Even as the number of tokens grows, this stays simpler, clearer, and easier to use. In addition, token A and token B can be combined easily and directly, in a form where their prices can be derived through Pi.

In practice, this way of organizing liquidity around the pairs with the strongest signals may bring users several benefits.

  • Prices of DEX tokens tend to move less, because each single trade has a smaller effect on the price
  • Slippage (the gap between the price when you place an order and the price of the actual trade) goes down, so trade results are easier to predict
  • Resistance to manipulation is higher, because moving the market costs more
  • Price discovery (finding a fair price through trading) becomes more reliable, especially as trading activity grows

These revisions were made after watching how Pioneers interact with the liquidity pools on Testnet, and they are being rolled out as an experiment. Like other Testnet features, this approach may change or be improved again while its use continues to be evaluated.

Domain verification as a trust signal for Pioneers

Domain verification was introduced, giving an extra signal that helps Pioneers judge whether a token can be trusted. Token issuers can now link their token to a verified domain name, and Pioneers can see whether a token was really issued by the side that controls that domain.

Before, a token was linked only to a specific blockchain address, which was hard for most Pioneers to read or judge.

Now, every token on the DEX and AMM interface must be linked to a verified domain, and the link can also be made from the domain side to the token. This makes it easier for Pioneers to check whether a token issuer that claims to represent a business, project, or service is really connected to the owner of the matching domain.

Note that this does not guarantee that a token is trustworthy or safe. It shows extra information on the screen so that Pioneers can judge for themselves. The responsibility for deciding what to trust lies with Pioneers, and the platform focuses on showing clearer, easier-to-understand signals that help people spot impersonation and false claims.

Ranking tokens by liquidity

On the DEX interface, Pi is testing a system that ranks tokens by liquidity rather than by market capitalization (the total market value of a token). In the current state of the system, this is considered the more useful measure and ranking method.

Ranking by market capitalization can be manipulated through the way supply and price are handled. For example, a developer can keep 99% of a token's total supply, put only 1% into circulation, and use the created scarcity to push the token's price up artificially. An artificially raised price inflates both the market capitalization and the fully diluted market capitalization (the value if all tokens were in circulation). Market capitalization is calculated as 'token price × circulating supply' and fully diluted market capitalization as 'token price × maximum supply', so a project can look far more valuable than its real value.

Ranking by liquidity addresses this problem by reflecting the amount of liquidity actually put into a token's pool. In this case it is measured in test Pi. This method values real commitment of funds. A token can rise in the ranking only by attracting more liquidity, not by inflating its price through manipulation. Liquidity can still be manipulated, but it requires committing a considerable amount of test Pi, so it is much harder and more costly. Rankings update automatically as liquidity changes.

This ranking system is now being tested, and it may change as more feedback and data come in.

With these updates, the DEX and AMM experience has been reworked. It is one improvement that better supports Pioneers as they get used to DeFi and learn about it, and better reaches the original aims.

Holiday commerce event

This holiday season, Pi Network is shining a light on commerce in the ecosystem through a community shopping initiative. In it, merchants who sell goods for Pi can spread their businesses through Pi Network, and Pioneers are encouraged to shop with Pi, both online and in person. The event features Mainnet (the live network) commerce apps and local merchants who accept Pi. It invites them to run their own holiday promotions on the Pi platform, and it gives Pioneers a simple way to find, shop, and share experiences across the ecosystem. Pi helps with visibility and coordination, creating a seasonal opportunity that supports real commerce, small businesses, and active participation across the Pi ecosystem.
