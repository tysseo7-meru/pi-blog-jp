Pi Founders Discussed Blockchain, KYC, Business Models, and Utility in the AI Era at Consensus 2026

Published: May 20, 2026

Categories: general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Dr. Chengdiao Fan and Dr. Nicolas Kokkalis, the founders of Pi, spoke at Consensus 2026 Miami. They each talked about their ideas on how blockchain (a technology that keeps records of transactions spread across many computers, so the records are hard to change) can support real uses, identity checks, trusted human participation, and business models that can last a long time in the age of AI (artificial intelligence).

Dr. Fan's talk, 'Aligning Web3, AI, and Blockchain for Utility,' took up a large problem for the whole crypto asset industry. The problem is that the design of tokens (units of digital value issued on a blockchain) and real product building are out of line with each other. Until now, tokens were often used to raise money or to sell at an early stage. Dr. Fan said the way of thinking needs to change, and tokens need to be treated as tools that support gaining users, user involvement with products, and long-term real uses. One example that put this idea into a real form is Pi Launchpad (a system on Pi for launching tokens for new projects).

The talk also showed how Pi's blockchain base, identity check system, and network active around the world can support products and businesses centered on real uses in the age of AI. In particular, it talked about Pi's way of thinking about ecosystem tokens (tokens used inside the Pi system) and the system for issuing them. The talk said tokens can be designed so that a product can gain real users, and those users can take part, give feedback, and use the tokens inside a real product experience.

Dr. Kokkalis took part in a panel discussion (a discussion with several speakers), 'How to Prove You're Human in an AI World (Without Doxing Yourself).' This discussion focused on one important problem that is appearing in the age of AI. The problem is this: AI systems can now create large numbers of bots (programs that act automatically), profiles, agents (AI programs that act for a person), and interactions that look real. In that situation, the problem is how to keep trust and how to check that real humans are taking part.

On the panel, Dr. Kokkalis explained that proof that a person is human is different depending on the situation. In some cases, it is necessary to know exactly who that person is. In other cases, it is enough to know that a human, not a bot, is doing a certain action, or to know that different real people are behind several actions. He also stressed that a way to check people while protecting privacy must not expose personal information that is not needed, and must be something that real people can keep using.

Dr. Kokkalis also touched on the importance of telling apart two things: AI agents that help real people and act for them, and fake activity that pretends to be human.

The two talks had a shared theme. As AI lowers the difficulty of creating things and taking part online, it may become harder to connect digital systems to real users, trusted participation, and meaningful real uses. Pi's Layer 1 blockchain (the main blockchain itself, which is the base), identity checks, payment base, non-custodial wallets (wallets where the user holds the keys to manage the assets themselves), smart contracts (programs that run automatically when set conditions are met), and community active around the world are all part of Pi's ongoing work to take on this problem.
