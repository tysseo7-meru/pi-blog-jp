Update On Mainnet Migration and Account Verification

Published: March 28, 2025

Categories: update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Until now, Pi accounts have relied on phone numbers, rather than email addresses, to tell users apart. This method was convenient at first, but it created challenges. Verification by SMS (short messages sent to a phone number) turned out to be unreliable and costly. Because the Pi community is spread around the world, SMS communication between Pioneers and the servers did not always work, due to differences in geographic limits of telecom regulations, carrier policies, and individuals' service plans and costs.

Given these challenges, and now that the network has more reliable ways to confirm that a person is genuine, such as KYC (identity verification) and liveness checks (checks that confirm the person is present in person), it is time to move away from relying mainly or only on phone numbers, toward verification by email two-factor authentication (2FA, a method that adds one more step of verification on top of the usual one). In the end, the aim is to support advanced authentication methods such as passkeys (a key system used instead of a password) and biometric authentication (verification using fingerprints, face, and the like). Email 2FA verification not only addresses the challenges above, but also raises security because it has two factors.

To get there, all users first need to have a registered trusted email address in order. However, some users may never have added an email address to their account, may have entered a wrong one, may no longer be able to use a past email address, or may be sharing an email address with a family member's Pi account.

Adding an email address to a Pi account is not done when the account is created. Because this is a major shift in account management and security, Pi had to verify through additional analysis before letting users make the change: how users added a trusted email address, how they verified the email address, and how they completed 2FA, in order to make sure of the completeness of these processes and the security of user accounts. In some cases, further action is needed, such as a liveness check or additional SMS verification. This required pausing migration until such system-level checks were done.

The good news is that migration has now resumed, and it will expand in stages as more email 2FA and system-level checks are completed. Pi Network says it is committed to a smooth and secure migration, and thanks everyone for their patience while everyone's account security is strengthened.
