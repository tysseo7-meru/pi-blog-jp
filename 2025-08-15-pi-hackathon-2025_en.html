Pi Hackathon 2025: Enhance Pi Utility in the Open Network Era

Published: August 15, 2025

Categories: announcement, hackathon

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Network has started Pi Hackathon 2025, the first hackathon (an event where developers compete by building intensively in a short period) in the ecosystem since the Open Network began. It asks developers to build and publish Pi apps that are useful in real-life situations using Pi, and to help shape the ecosystem. Teams compete for a total prize pool of up to 160,000 Pi.

With the Open Network's external connections, there is now an opportunity to further expand Pi's current usefulness through practical apps that bring real uses and value to everyday users, including people who have never mined Pi before.

Building on the app-building momentum created at Pi2Day 2025, this hackathon encourages developers to create tools, apps, and experiences that make Pi easier to use and closer to daily life around the world.

The challenge: build apps that increase Pi's usefulness

The challenge is to build Pi apps that increase Pi's usefulness by letting people engage with and take part in the ecosystem. The theme is broad and open, in a form that widely invites creativity. An app may focus on any kind of use, but it must follow the Mainnet listing requirements and bring real usefulness to the Pi community.

Developers are encouraged to do the following.

  • Be inventive and creative, based on users' real needs
  • Build open source projects using Pi Open Source (PiOS, Pi's open source system) and make use of community contributions
  • Use Pi App Studio and the Brainstorm app as a starting point to turn early ideas into full apps
  • Use AI technology, including coding support, to support app development and improvement

Prizes

The winning teams will receive the following prizes.

  • 1st place: 75,000 Pi
  • 2nd place: 45,000 Pi
  • 3rd place: 15,000 Pi
  • Honorable mentions (up to 5 teams): 5,000 Pi each

How to take part

To take part in the hackathon, do the following.

  • Register with the official Hackathon Registration Form and join the email list to receive the latest information
  • Build as a team and work together using Pi App Studio, the Developer Portal (the registration and management screen for developers), and shared materials

Schedule

  • August 15 — Registration and team formation begin: participants can register for the hackathon and start forming or joining a team
  • August 21 — The hackathon officially begins
  • September 19 — Midpoint check: teams are encouraged to submit a midpoint check form. Projects that submit may receive extra guidance, a chance to be introduced to the Pi community, and feedback before the final judging
  • October 15 — Final submission deadline: all final submissions are due, including the app listing in the Developer Portal, the demo video, and the submission form

Team size

There is no limit on the number of team members. Developers are encouraged to work together, form focused teams, and build fewer but higher-quality apps. By October 15, each team must submit the final list of team members in the final submission form. Also, to be eligible to receive a prize, every team member must have passed Pi KYC (identity verification).

Submission requirements

For an app to count as a valid submission to Pi Hackathon 2025, the following two steps must be completed.

  • Submit the Hackathon Submission Google Form together with a demo video (the form will be shared as the deadline approaches)
  • Submit the app in the Pi Developer Portal

Both steps are needed for a submission to count as complete. Details and links will be shared with participants as the deadline approaches.

1. Recommended: midpoint submission (by September 19, 2025)

To check progress and provide support, teams are encouraged to do a midpoint check. It is not required for the final evaluation, but completing this step may make a team eligible for extra guidance and feedback, and may give it early user attention and an introduction to the community as the submission date approaches.

The midpoint submission may ask for the following.

  • The app's name and URL
  • Preview images of the app being developed
  • A short development plan text that summarizes the planned features and functions

The submission form and the specific requirements will be delivered directly to all registered participants before the submission deadline.

2. Final submission form and demo video

The final submission form will be delivered directly to all registered participants before the deadline.

Each team must make a demo video that introduces the final version of its app. The video must be published on YouTube or another video sharing service, and its link must be included in the submission form.

The demo video is to cover the following.

  • A short introduction explaining the app and its purpose
  • A demonstration of the app's functions and how its screens are used
  • An explanation of the users the app is for
  • The problem or need the app addresses
  • How Pi is built into the user experience (payments, login, and so on)

The demo is to be concise and easy to understand, and no longer than 3 minutes in total.

3. Final app submission in the Developer Portal

To submit an app through the Developer Portal, complete the following steps.

I. Enter the hackathon code. Open Pi Browser, go to the Developer Portal, and tap the "Hackathon" button on the main "Develop" page. Enter the hackathon code "hackathon25" and tap "Join". This records the Developer Portal account as a hackathon participant.

II. Create and register a Mainnet app. If you already have a Mainnet app in progress, choose the app you want to submit to the hackathon and complete as much of the Developer Portal Checklist as possible. If you are starting new, tap "New App" in the Developer Portal and follow the usual app creation flow. Choose "Mainnet" as the app type. Note that registering a Mainnet app still requires a link with the testnet version. Complete as much of the Developer Portal Checklist as possible.

III. Submit the app to the hackathon. When you enter the code during an eligible hackathon period, a new "Submit to Hackathon" button appears when you reach step 10 of the Checklist (the "Add a PiNet subdomain" step). Tapping "Submit to Hackathon" automatically submits the app to the hackathon category. Submitted apps will be listed, within a few weeks, on the Hackathon Submissions page in the Pi Apps tab of Pi Browser.

IV. The app wallet and ecosystem settings. At this point, no wallet setup is needed for a hackathon submission. Teams may complete this step after the event, especially if they are chosen as winners or move forward to an official listing in the ecosystem.

V. App eligibility. Each user can submit up to 2 Mainnet apps to the hackathon. Apps must follow all the required Mainnet Listing Guidelines, must be original, and must focus on Pi's usefulness.

4. App judging criteria

All apps are judged based on the following criteria.

  • Fit with the Mainnet Listing Guidelines
  • Fit with Pi's usefulness and the needs of the whole community
  • Technical completeness and innovation
  • UI/UX (the screens and ease of use), usability, and ease of operation
  • Long-term usefulness on the Pi Mainnet blockchain
  • Originality and distinctive use cases

Apps must also meet the following conditions.

  • Have an English name
  • Have a logo that follows the trademark guidelines
  • Have an accurate description in English
  • Include preview/demo images showing the app's screens and how it is used
  • Be connected with PiNet
  • Include a privacy policy

The Brainstorm app and Pi App Studio

Participants can use the Brainstorm app on Pi Browser to share prototypes, connect with others, and raise awareness within the Pi community.

If you made an app prototype using Pi App Studio or the Developer Portal, you can link it directly to a project listing on Brainstorm. This helps you present your idea, gather feedback early, and find potential team members and collaborators for the hackathon.

To link an app to Brainstorm, follow these steps.

  • Open the Brainstorm app from Pi Browser
  • Tap the "Propose" button in the navigation bar at the bottom
  • Follow the instructions on the page and enter the project name and a short summary
  • After submitting, scroll to the bottom of the project page and tap "Link this project with an Application on the Developer Portal"
  • You can then choose from the apps you already made in Pi App Studio or the Developer Portal

Once linked, the project becomes visible to other Brainstorm users, so more Pioneers can see the idea, take interest, and get involved.

A call to developers

Pi Hackathon 2025 is an opportunity for developers to make a meaningful contribution to the Pi ecosystem by building real apps that encourage Pi's usefulness and community participation. Whether you are an experienced developer or just starting out, it is a chance to build something that has an impact, work with others, and show your ideas to the Pi community around the world.

The network states that it is ready to welcome the creativity, innovation, and enthusiasm that participants bring to this event.
