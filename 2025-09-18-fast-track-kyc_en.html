Fast Track KYC Enables Earlier Wallet Activation for Ecosystem Participation for New Users

Published: September 18, 2025

Categories: announcement, general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Network introduced Fast Track KYC, a new feature designed and enabled by building more AI into the KYC (identity verification) process. New Pioneers and people who are not yet users can complete Fast Track KYC to take part in the Mainnet (live network) ecosystem earlier than before. This update builds on the widening of who can activate a wallet. People who have completed KYC can now activate a wallet even before completing their migration to Mainnet, and the network continues its mission of improving accessibility while keeping strong safety standards.

Earlier access to the ecosystem

Traditionally, new users had to complete at least 30 mining sessions (sessions of mining Pi) before they could submit a standard KYC application. This created a natural delay between joining the network and being able to interact with the Mainnet ecosystem. With Fast Track KYC, that barrier is removed.

Now, Pioneers with fewer than 30 mining sessions (including people who are not currently users) can go through the early identity verification process and become eligible to activate a Mainnet wallet. If eligible, users can see this option directly inside the Pi Wallet app. They can start KYC, and once verified, they can immediately access the Pi Mainnet wallet and its uses.

What Fast Track KYC makes possible

Once a Mainnet wallet is activated, new Pioneers can start using Pi apps, local commerce, and participation in ecosystem events. However, Fast Track KYC does not enable migration to Mainnet. The mined balance is not moved until the user becomes fully eligible (for example, by completing at least 30 mining sessions) and completes the Mainnet Checklist, which includes the standard KYC process.

Fast Track KYC also answers the identity verification needed to activate a Mainnet wallet, as a unique solution inside the Pi ecosystem. It does not rely on outside services or on the complexity of the full Mainnet migration process. It provides a safe and reliable alternative path, of equal standing, for new users to verify their identity and activate a wallet. This is a major milestone for Pi: verified new users and non-users can quickly activate a wallet through Pi's own base. Outside providers such as Banxa continue to offer an onboarding option, but they are now one of two paths, and no longer the only solution for new Pioneers.

Fast Track KYC does not guarantee a quick 'pass' through KYC, and it does not lower the quality of KYC acceptance. Applications are rejected if they do not meet the standard KYC criteria. Rather, Fast Track KYC helps people activate a Mainnet wallet earlier, while, because of its automated nature, its criteria may be even more careful.

The future of KYC

By verifying new users and giving them earlier access to the Mainnet ecosystem without affecting migration, Fast Track KYC allows further observation, analysis, adjustment, and improvement. After repeated improvement in this use, the technology behind Fast Track KYC may later be built into the standard KYC process. That could make that process faster, clear the migration backlog, and save the network's human verification work overall.

Making the network stronger for everyone

Fast Track KYC supports Pi's wider idea of building an accessible, utility-centered digital ecosystem powered by verified, real users. By letting new Pioneers take part earlier, this feature widens the range of verified people who can get involved, supports developers, and speeds up the use and testing of ecosystem apps.

Overall, identity verification through KYC is important for the health of the network, for compliance with laws, and for readiness for integration by services that need identity verification. Pi's early idea, from several years ago, of building a blockchain network verified through KYC, and its achievement of more than 14.82 million people who have migrated and been KYC-verified on the Pi Mainnet blockchain, are now also reflected in recent industry movement toward verified, permissioned standards, such as ERC-3643, which builds identity verification and compliance systems directly into tokens on public networks.

In other news, KYC became available for users in Syria. Following Executive Order 14312, which ended the U.S. government's Syria sanctions program, and in accordance with all applicable laws and rules, Pi's KYC service became available to eligible individuals in Syria.
