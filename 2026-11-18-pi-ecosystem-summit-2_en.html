Pi Ecosystem Summit Day 2: AI & Ecosystem

Published: November 18, 2026

Categories: announcement, developer

Original article

The Pi Network Ecosystem Summit reached its second day on November 18, 2026. Following the announcements about the developer platform (the base that developers use to build apps) and the technical demos on Day 1, Day 2 focused on how Pi's base supports AI, human participation, and real-world uses.

The content of this day's video program is as follows. The video was released at 8:00 a.m. PST / 4:00 p.m. UTC.

  • Opening remarks: Founder Nicolas Kokkalis
  • Pi DataLab demo: A preview of a new system that connects identity-verified users to data work that needs human judgment
  • Pi SoloHost AI skill demo: How to use an AI agent (AI that receives instructions and carries out work) to prepare an existing app so it can be published through SoloHost on Pi Desktop
  • CiDi Games technical demo: An explanation of how user authentication, Pi Payments, and the Pi Ads SDK (a development kit for putting ads into an app) are built into CiDi's gaming platform
  • Conversation with Resonant: Content in which Founder Chengdiao Fan and J, a representative of Resonant, talked about building on Pi, AI data made by people, and chances to take part in the AI economy

Pi DataLab: Connecting AI demand with a network of identity-verified people

Pi Network demonstrated Pi DataLab in practice for the first time. Pi DataLab is a new human-in-the-loop system (a way of working where human judgment is included in the process of AI work). It is designed to connect companies, AI developers, and other organizations that need high-quality human input with Pi's global network of identity-verified Pi users.

As AI systems improve, demand is growing for data made by people, feedback, and judgment to train, evaluate, and improve AI. With Pi DataLab, organizations can create and fund projects that need human input. This includes data labeling (adding labels to data), ranking, evaluation, physical AI data (AI data collected in the real world), and other work that depends on human judgment.

This system is designed so that the requesting side can decide the work content, set participation conditions and quality control methods, distribute work to eligible participants, and check and receive completed results.

On the participant side, Pioneers (Pi users) can look for work they can take, check the instructions and reward, complete tasks, and receive Pi for eligible contributions. Systems such as qualification screening and evaluation of contributors' records will also be built in, to keep the quality of submitted data and to build contributor reputations that will help with future work opportunities.

Pi DataLab is built on Pi's existing experience in coordinating large-scale human participation. Pi's KYC (identity verification) base has already coordinated more than 526 million verification tasks completed by more than one million human verifiers, showing that distributed human contributions can be organized at large scale.

In this day's demo, the central flow of Pi DataLab was introduced: creating and managing projects, finding and completing work, and handling submitted results.

More broadly, Pi DataLab aims to open Pi's distributed human base to outside demand. It makes new forms of human participation in creating and evaluating AI data possible, and gives Pioneers a chance to take part in the growing AI economy. Rewards for participation through Pi DataLab are paid in tokens, including Pi or tokens issued on Pi, not in fiat currency (government-issued money) like traditional Web2 platforms.

Packaging apps for SoloHost with help from AI

This day's program also demonstrated how developers can use an AI skill to make it easier to prepare an existing app for publication through SoloHost. SoloHost is a system for publishing an app in a form that other users can install and use.

With SoloHost, developers can bring apps they run themselves (self-hosted apps, apps that developers run on their own) to Pi Desktop, opening a new route to reach more than 420,000 Node operators (people who run a Node, a computer that helps run the network) and other Pi Desktop users with their products.

Any work that can be packaged as a Docker image (a distribution format that bundles an app together with the environment it needs to run) may be able to run on SoloHost. This means developers can package an existing app with the right settings, distribute it through the SoloHost environment to reach Pi Desktop users, and in the future it may reach even more Pioneers.

Pi recently introduced a new AI skill in the public SoloHost GitHub repository (a place where program files are stored and shared) to make packaging work easier.

Developers give the public Docker image of an existing app to a compatible AI agent, such as Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex. The agent asks what functions the app should provide, creates the two required setting files, docker-compose.yml and config_options.yml, and checks their content. Developers can then use these files to apply for listing on SoloHost.

In this day's demo, this flow was shown in practice, introducing how an AI agent can help developers prepare for SoloHost while reducing manual technical work.

CiDi Games: Building Pi into gaming apps

Following the Day 1 conversation about CiDi Games' experience developing on Pi, this day's technical demo showed in detail how the company's gaming platform builds in Pi's developer tools and base. CiDi Games' experience also shows the breadth of Pi's tools, which support the whole life of an app, from building and integration to payments, earning money, gaining users, and continued use.

The demo covered Pi user authentication (checking who a user is), Pi Payments for transactions within supported gaming experiences, and use of the Pi Ads SDK to build advertising revenue (earning money from ads) into an app.

Together, these integrations show how developers can bring Pi platform functions into their own apps and support user experience and business models within the ecosystem.

Other new features for developers

Pi released several more features to support developers building apps in the ecosystem.

Wider support for programming languages: The range of developer tools was expanded to support a wider range of programming languages and frameworks (structures for development), including Laravel, Go, FastAPI, Node/Express, and Ruby. This gives developers more choices when building Pi functions into apps made with technology they already use.

Unified documentation: Unified developer documentation was also released, bringing together materials on app setup, authentication, payments, and testing in one place. This makes it easier for developers to build apps that connect with Pi.

A completely new hackathon starts tomorrow

Day 2 of the Ecosystem Summit showed how developers can use Pi's base to build apps and services that involve AI, human participation, and practical use. From Pi DataLab's system to connect human contributors with AI-related work, to SoloHost development steps that make it easier to distribute self-run AI agents and access distributed computing (using many computers together), to CiDi Games' technical integration making use of a wide range of Pi functions, these demonstrations show additional ways that Pi's existing resources support ecosystem growth and practical use.

The summit will reach its final day tomorrow, November 19, 2026, when Pi Next, Pi's completely new hackathon (an event where people compete to develop intensively in a short period), will officially begin. Pi Next has a total prize pool of 200,000 USDT and up to 20 million Pi prepared as a joint marketing incentive (a reward to support promotion together). Developers will have a chance to put the tools and features introduced through the summit into practice and build apps for the Pi ecosystem. The full rules and steps for the hackathon, including how to take part, will be announced tomorrow.
