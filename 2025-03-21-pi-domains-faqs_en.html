The .pi Domains Auction: Stats, More Details, and FAQs

Published: March 21, 2025

Categories: general, pi-day

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

The .pi domains auction is a step to expand the Pi ecosystem (the group of apps and services connected to Pi). There is strong participation from Pioneers, businesses, and developers. As a platform-level utility (a basic use that supports the whole system), the domains auction uses the collective resources of the Pi community and the connection to the outside through the Open Network (the live network that connects to the outside) to further Pi's role in real commerce and digital interaction.

More than 100,000 bids were placed in less than one week. The auction is creating real involvement and is strengthening Pi's role as a currency backed by function and use for all Pioneers. Demand for .pi domains shows that an ecosystem of Pi-powered apps, businesses, and online services is growing, using blockchain-based (using a blockchain, a shared record kept by many computers) domain ownership for trademarks, branding, ease of use, and commerce.

The main figures at this time are as follows. These figures are only for Pioneers on the mainnet (the live main network) who can bid.

  • Bids exceeded 200,000.
  • Individual bidders exceeded 40,000.
  • Domains with active bids exceeded 95,000.
  • Active bids in the auction exceeded 2.9 million Pi.
  • The current highest bid is 30,000 Pi.

For more details on .pi domains, readers are directed to read the Pi Day announcement. In this article, Pi published a new policy document for the domains auction, answered some frequently asked questions from the community, and clarified the rules for Pioneers and businesses who may need more details in order to take part.

Frequently asked questions

The answers to frequently asked questions are below.

General use and functions

  • Question: How does a .pi domain compare to a traditional website domain in Web2 (the traditional internet)? Answer: A .pi domain works in a similar way to a traditional domain and works as a unique digital identifier (a name that identifies something online) within the Pi ecosystem. It allows businesses, apps, and individuals to build a clear online identity (a way of showing who they are online) or digital storefront in a Web3 (a decentralized internet based on technologies such as blockchain) social network, and part of how it is obtained, transferred, and operated depends heavily on the Pi blockchain.
  • Question: Will a .pi domain work outside the Pi Browser? Answer: The .pi extension of Pi domains works inside the Pi Browser, and other wallets and browsers may connect with this feature over time, in the same way as other crypto domains. For compatibility with traditional browsers such as Chrome and Safari, all .pi domains also work through the extension ".pinet.com". So if a domain is "joe.pi" in the Pi Browser or a compatible third-party service, it can also be found as "joe.pinet.com" from any browser. There is no guarantee or promise that .pi domains will be adopted by traditional browsers.
  • Question: Can I bid for my username as a domain? Answer: Yes, if it is available.
  • Question: Where do the proceeds from domain sales go? Answer: All Pi received from selling .pi domains will be used to support the Pi ecosystem, including developer programs and events.

Domain reservation for PiNet apps

  • Question: Can developers who have already built a Pi app reserve a domain? Answer: Pi community apps that follow the Ecosystem listing guidelines and complete PiNet migration by May 28, 2025 can secure their own domain without bidding, subject to certain requirements and limits. For details, readers are directed to read the article on this topic.

Bidding and how the auction works

  • Question: Can I bid on multiple domains at the same time? Answer: Yes, you can bid on multiple domains at the same time.
  • Question: Can I cancel or change my bid after placing it? Answer: Yes, for Pioneers you can. If you placed a bid using the claimable balance feature (a feature that lets you use a balance you can claim), you can withdraw it at any time before the auction ends. However, if you placed a bid by a normal payment through a multiplexed wallet address (an address that combines several payments) because you are not yet a Pioneer (for example, from an exchange), refunds will be processed after the auction ends on June 28, 2025.
  • Question: What happens if someone bids at the last moment? Answer: All auctions are scheduled to end on June 28, 2025. However, if a new bid is placed within the last 24 hours of that domain's auction period, or the highest bid is lowered or removed, that specific auction will automatically be extended by 24 hours. The auction rules, including this rule, may be changed at the company's sole discretion (its decision alone).
  • Question: If I win in the domains auction, when do I receive ownership? Answer: Winners gain ownership immediately after the auction ends. The domain may have future yearly registration and maintenance fees, similar to and on the same scale as traditional internet domain fees.
  • Question: After the auction, how do I retrieve the domains I own? Answer: At the end of the auction, you will be able to see a list of domains you successfully purchased on the Domain Auction page or app. Domain ownership is linked to the wallet address used for bidding.
  • Question: Can the bidding and auction rules change? Answer: Yes, the company reserves the right to change or modify these rules at any time, in its sole and absolute discretion, with or without notice to participants. For example, the auction currently allows Pioneers to withdraw bids at any time, giving maximum flexibility. However, if this proves not to be effective in practice, it may be sensible to move to a more binding bidding system where bids cannot be withdrawn before the auction ends, in order to prevent misuse and make sure things are fair.

Ownership, transfer, and renewal

  • Question: Can I resell or transfer my .pi domain in the future? Answer: Yes, .pi domains can be resold or transferred to others, following standard domain ownership practices. Use of a domain is separately bound by applicable laws and regulations, such as trademarks and copyrights.
  • Question: What happens if I do not renew my .pi domain after the first ownership period? Answer: If you do not renew your .pi domain, it will eventually become available for others to register, in the same way as a standard domain expiry in Web2.

This list of frequently asked questions will develop as more Pioneers become involved with .pi domains. If you have more questions, you are directed to visit domains.pi or domains.pinet.com through the Pi Browser to explore or take part in the auction.
