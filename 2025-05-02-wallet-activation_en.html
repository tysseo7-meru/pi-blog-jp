Expanding Accessibility to Pi Mainnet Ecosystem Through New Wallet Activation Opportunities

Published: May 2, 2025

Categories: announcement

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Inclusiveness (accepting many people without distinction) is at the center of Pi's vision, and Pi's Mainnet (the live network) ecosystem has become much more accessible for people who have completed identity verification. This includes Pioneers who have fully completed KYC (identity verification) and Pioneers who have completed it tentatively.

With the new Mainnet wallet activation feature, Pioneers who have completed identity verification can activate their own wallet on the Pi Mainnet blockchain. It covers people who have fully completed KYC and most people who have completed it tentatively. This makes it possible to take part in the Pi Mainnet ecosystem, and widens who is eligible.

Mainnet migration is continuing, and the network is putting effort into speeding it up and completing it, but migration takes more time. That is because, in addition to identity verification, complex processing and analysis must be run, and users must do certain tasks, to make sure the correct amount of Pi is safely distributed to genuine Pioneers who did not cheat. By separating Mainnet migration from being able to have a Mainnet wallet, access is widened to millions of people, while keeping it true that all participants in Pi Mainnet have completed identity verification through KYC or KYB (the business and company equivalent of identity verification).

Eligible Pioneers can get started by going to the Pi Wallet app inside Pi Browser.

In addition, people who are not yet Pi users can now also access the Pi Mainnet blockchain and ecosystem in a much easier way, through another new feature released today. This feature can be used, in supported regions, through third-party services that work with Pi. After passing similar KYC requirements at those third-party services, non-users too can easily get a wallet on the Pi Mainnet blockchain and take part in the Pi Mainnet ecosystem. An important note for Pioneers: passing third-party KYC only makes Mainnet wallet creation possible; for the reasons above, it does not affect a Pioneer's Mainnet migration.

Also, Pi is considering expanding the authority to perform KYC to third parties that meet the same and equivalent standards. However, a third-party service, by its own business judgment, may not have the same scale of reach or wide regional coverage as Pi Network. For that reason, any third-party service Pi is trying is in an experimental beta (a trial before the official version) stage. The first such third-party service, an on-ramp (an entry service for buying crypto assets with government-issued currency), recently integrated with Pi after passing the KYB requirements. This service has its own regional limits, and cannot be used in all countries and regions. The integration has been tested and is in a state where Pioneers can start using it, but because it is in a beta testing stage, further repeated improvements may be needed to scale it to handle Pi's tens of millions of users, and it may be paused or adjusted in the future. Pioneers can provide feedback about this service through Pi's official communication channels and apps. Other on-ramps are also being invited to apply for KYB verification.

Before this feature, the only way for a non-user to take part in the Pi Mainnet ecosystem was to start from zero after registering an account: do at least 30 mining sessions (sessions of mining), pass Pi KYC, and complete the Mainnet migration steps and migrate, which could take a long time overall. This feature greatly reduces the steps and time a non-user needs to receive a Mainnet wallet and engage directly with Pi's utility. In this way, more people can be welcomed into the Pi Mainnet ecosystem sooner, while protecting the network's security and KYC standards.

Also, unlike non-users who only trade Pi on centralized exchanges and do not actually own Pi on the Mainnet blockchain and cannot directly take part in and contribute to the Pi Mainnet ecosystem, non-users welcomed through this feature own a Mainnet wallet on the Pi Mainnet blockchain. Because of that, they can take part directly in Pi apps, local commerce in Pi, and events such as the .pi domain auction, and the possibility of ecosystem contributions from non-users widens.

In addition, this feature shows a new change toward a more open direction. After KYB verification and checking the standards of third-party services' KYC procedures, Pi has, for the first time, allowed a third party to perform KYC for Pioneers outside Pi's own KYC system. This expansion of KYC authority to a third-party service for wallet creation is also part of the effort to raise accessibility and inclusiveness across Pi Mainnet as a whole. This third-party service is Banxa, the on-ramp mentioned earlier, and it is listed on Pi's official web page as a KYB-verified service.

Widening access to the Mainnet Pi ecosystem strengthens Pi's utility, by allowing more Pioneers and non-users to own and use Pi directly on the blockchain and engage with Pi in real apps. It also makes sure of a smooth participation experience for people outside the Pi ecosystem, while protecting network security and KYC standards.

At a wider level, this integration benefits the whole ecosystem. More Pioneers having Mainnet wallets means a larger base of participants for developers and ecosystem apps, producing more transactions, tests, and feedback, and speeding up Pi's development and real-world use.

As always, you are asked not to use third-party services that claim to work with Pi but are not listed on this KYB service's web page. Using them can expose you to the risk of fraud.
