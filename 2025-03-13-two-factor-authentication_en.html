Two-Factor Authentication For Pi Migration Wallet Helps Ensure Pioneer Account Security

Published: March 13, 2025

Categories: general, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

On March 13, 2025, Pi Network released a two-factor authentication feature (2FA, a method that adds one more step of verification on top of the usual one). Some Pioneers need to finish 2FA before their Pi is safely migrated to the Mainnet (the live network) blockchain. This feature protects and confirms Pioneers' Pi Wallets through the verification of a trusted email address, and supports the security of Pioneers' accounts and their Pi.

Some Pioneers already have a trusted email address that they can use to start 2FA to confirm their migration wallet. Others need to set up a trusted email address before they can finish the 2FA to confirm their wallet. Setting up a trusted email is done by completing a liveness check (a check that confirms the person is present in person), so that the email address is certain to have been provided by the account owner. Guidance for this appears in the mining app. Currently, only Pioneers who have completed KYC (identity verification), including tentative KYC, can add a trusted email address. A trusted email address is also used for other important account verifications, such as account recovery. For that reason, it is essential to add an email address that you own and trust. If you provide a random email address that you cannot access, you will not be able to complete verifications. As a result, in the future you may be unable to confirm your wallet to migrate your Pi, or to recover your account.

Once a trusted email address is added, those Pioneers gradually receive guidance after a mining session ends, and in some cases email notifications as well, asking them to finish 2FA to confirm their migration wallet. Alternatively, Pioneers can also finish the 2FA wallet confirmation through the Mainnet Checklist.

On the 2FA screen, Pioneers must confirm access to their migration wallet (or create a new wallet and change to it). After that, they receive another email to verify this confirmation.

The only emails Pioneers receive are from "[email protected]". To avoid phishing (a trick that uses fake screens or emails to steal information) and scam attempts, do not trust any other email.

Pi balances returned during the migration pending period

For the security of accounts and Pi, certain Pioneers who migrated Pi to Mainnet in the past few weeks but are still within the 14-day pending period need to complete the 2FA requirement above. If they do not, their Pi will be safely returned to the mining app. The design of the 14-day pending period after migration was also for purposes exactly like this. Once these Pioneers complete 2FA, their migration, for the original balance or a slightly larger balance, is put back in the queue toward the Mainnet blockchain.

The 2FA and Pi "return" features are there to ensure the security of Pioneers' Pi and accounts as much as possible. Because blockchain transactions cannot be changed and Pi Wallet is non-custodial (a form where you manage your own assets), these features provide extra care and make sure Pi is sent to the legitimate wallet owner.

Pioneers who completed 2FA before the return are not subject to the Pi return. Once 2FA is done, Pioneers whose migration was paused and whose Pi was returned will have their migration started again.

Important notes about the Pi return and the Mainnet migration process are as follows.

  • The 14-day pending period is a period in which Pi is frozen and cannot be used on the Mainnet blockchain, and it is an essential part of the migration process. One reason is to give time to ensure the safety of Pioneers' Pi and the accuracy of the migration before it becomes permanently irreversible on the Mainnet blockchain.
  • Some Pioneers whose Pi was returned may see their migrated Pi balance go back to "Unverified Balance" rather than "Transferable Balance". This is because the amount shown in the Transferable Balance is a conservative estimate for display purposes, while the actual amount being migrated goes through advanced calculations that usually take a long time to compute. Because of this, the amount of Pi a Pioneer receives in the next migration does not decrease. After the migration completes safely, the Pioneer receives the same amount of Pi as the original migration amount (or slightly more, because more mining sessions are included).

Because of the 2FA requirement for wallet confirmation and the return of Pi for some accounts during the 14-day period, the circulating supply may decrease. However, as more Pi is put back in the queue and migrated again, the circulating supply will change accordingly.

If you have completed all the steps of the Mainnet Checklist and are waiting for the migration process to finish, you need to add a trusted email address and, once you receive the guidance, complete 2FA as soon as possible.
