Pi Node Decentralized Computing Case Study

Published: March 5, 2026

Categories: developer, update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Network is exploring the power of its worldwide network of Pi Nodes (computers that take part in the network) to support decentralized AI training and computing work.

Pi Nodes have long been designed to play a role that is more than protecting the ledger (the record of transactions). The Pi blockchain gains from the decentralized nature of many Nodes spread around the world. But Pi's ledger itself uses energy efficiently. It does not need all the computing resources of such a large decentralized Node network. Because of this, a pool of unused computing power is created. This can make new uses possible, such as decentralized cloud computing (a system where many computers share the processing work). A third party that needs computing power to train an AI model can use these unused resources of Pi Node operators who choose to take part, and can pay the operators in cryptocurrency. Utility on Pi Nodes is an important part of Pi's utility, together with Pi apps, platform-level utility, and local commerce.

This Node utility aims to respond to two problems that are appearing in the AI era.

  • The limits of centralized computing: This includes problems such as limits on data centers (large buildings that hold many computers), concentration of energy, catastrophic forgetting (a phenomenon where earlier knowledge is lost during learning), and global-state bottlenecks (a blockage in processing).
  • The growing demand for computing power because of AI: As AI models and agents (AI that acts on its own) improve, and as the AI economy grows, the demand for computing resources grows. Society will need to provide an amount of computing power never seen before to support the AI economy. It needs to create new computing power, and use existing unused computing power as much as possible. Scattered and unused computing power depends on a decentralized network, and on technology that gathers it, coordinates it, and expands its scale, in order to be productive.

Pi is in a unique position to solve these two challenges. Pi itself is already a decentralized network. It has more than 421,000 Nodes (equal to more than 1 million CPUs) that make decentralized computing possible. In addition, Pi has tens of millions of participants who have finished identity verification by KYC. They can take part and provide human-in-the-loop support (a system where humans are involved in the process) for related AI learning processes, in exchange for rewards in cryptocurrency. When this is added to the computing power from Pi Nodes, it can provide a unique resource for AI systems: human input that is large in scale and real. It further completes a single, combined service for AI clients.

When these elements come together, a new approach to AI infrastructure (the basic systems that AI depends on) is created. It lets a decentralized system bring production into its own network. It lets people contribute directly to AI production processes, and receive rewards in return through blockchain-based payments.

Last October, Pi successfully completed a proof-of-concept project (a trial to check whether an idea can work in practice) with OpenMind. In that project, a small group of Pi Node operators ran image recognition work for the company.

The OpenMind Case Study

OpenMind is developing an operating system (basic software) and an open-source protocol (common rules that are made public) for robots to think, learn, and work together. It is like Android OS for robots. Like other physical AI efforts (AI with a real body), OpenMind needs large computing power to train, evaluate, and run its models.

To test whether Pi's decentralized computing can work in practice, OpenMind developed a container (a package that holds a work environment together) that can request computing work from individual computers. The team shared this container with volunteer Pi Node operators. The operators downloaded it to their machines and ran it.

OpenMind then sent work through the container. It asked the computers to process different images using OpenMind's AI image recognition model. This is a use case where training for image recognition is essential for OpenMind's robots to interact with the real world. The goal was to find as many individual objects as possible in these images, using the computing power of the volunteer Pi Node operators.

As a result, the proof-of-concept project was run successfully. The work was sent correctly to external testers (the volunteer Pi Node operators), and valid results were returned to OpenMind. The use case was proven. A Pi Node can choose to take part and run computations that a third party, unrelated to its blockchain duties, defines and requests, and it can return meaningful results to the third-party client.

The main figures from the test are as follows.

  • 7 volunteer Pi Node operators took part in the test
  • The distributed processing flow worked successfully from start to finish
  • The jobs that were sent received acknowledgments from 7 workers within 1 second
  • Inference results (the results produced by the AI model) were returned from multiple workers within 4 seconds
  • The results included correct object detections. They included the expected labels, such as bus and person, and bounding boxes (frames that show the position of an object)
  • Jobs were sent successfully to the machines of external testers, and valid detection results were returned. This confirmed the reliability of both the distributed delivery path and the result return path

Overall, this experiment confirmed that distributed Pi Nodes can run work related to AI and return useful results quickly.

Next Steps

Decentralized AI training is still at the research stage in the world. Much work by researchers and interested companies around the world needs to continue to explore whether, and how, a move from fully centralized AI training to more distributed methods is possible. This research is directly related to the challenges stated earlier: responding to the structural limits of centralized computing, using scattered and unused computing power for AI, and creating chances for individuals to take part in a meaningful way in AI-led production.

By evaluating how unused Node computing power can support external AI work, and by providing computing power and real human input together, this effort may provide an alternative infrastructure option. AI companies and startups can consider it when they look for solutions to their AI training needs.

Overall, Pi's research into this Node utility adds to Pi's view of the future of blockchain and AI. In that view, decentralized infrastructure contributes to essential parts of the future economy, and supports fair participation and distribution.
