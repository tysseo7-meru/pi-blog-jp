KYC and Mainnet Migration Updates: More Pioneers Unblocked and New Authentication Exploration

Published: January 30, 2026

Categories: update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi has released a technical update that lets millions of Pioneers (Pi users) complete Mainnet migration (moving to the live blockchain). Another update, which will let hundreds of thousands of Pioneers submit a KYC (identity check) application, will also be provided within the next few weeks.

Pi will also release a palm print authentication feature (a way to confirm a person by the pattern of their palm) that is in the beta testing stage. It may help the system handle additional liveness checks (checks that the person is really operating the app), account verification, and more. Meanwhile, the distribution of rewards to KYC validators (the people who review identity checks) continues to progress, and it remains on track for rollout by the end of March 2026.

These updates not only let more Pioneers take part in the Mainnet ecosystem. They also strengthen Pi's two big strengths: 1) the scale of the community, and 2) a Mainnet blockchain where everyone is fully identity-verified.

Overall, Pi has now reached 16 million Pioneers migrated to the Mainnet, which sets Pi apart as a large-scale identity-verified blockchain.

Unblocking Mainnet migration for millions of Pioneers

About 2.5 million Pioneers, who were previously blocked because of additional security and compliance checks in specific regions, can now fully migrate to the Mainnet.

For the people unblocked by this update, if they are active in mining (the action of earning Pi in the app) and have completed the Mainnet Checklist, the system will automatically migrate their transferable balance.

The article also explains why migration and KYC are unblocked in batches. Different groups of Pioneers are held up in the migration or KYC process by different special cases — for example, the need for additional checks, the evaluation and decision of Tentative KYC status, and account security measures before migration. Each special case needs its own technical solution to resolve the undetermined state of the affected accounts, so that as many genuine Pioneers as possible can pass while bad actors and policy violators are caught. When such an individual technical solution is completed and released, the group of Pioneers it unblocks is unblocked. For that reason, the pace of unblocking is set by the release of these technical solutions, unlike the general, ongoing KYC and migration process for most Pioneers.

More than 700,000 more Pioneers will soon be able to submit KYC applications

Within the next few weeks, more than 700,000 Pioneers who were previously not eligible will be able to submit KYC applications. Meanwhile, the network continues to process these accounts to ensure the integrity of large-scale Mainnet migration. While this processing continues, the applications of these unblocked Pioneers may need additional analysis for a KYC decision.

When the update is released, people who were previously blocked in KYC are asked to open the Pi app and check whether they can move forward. Submitting KYC as soon as possible helps put an account in position for the next stage.

Exploring palm print authentication in KYC for further authentication

Pi will test palm print authentication as a beta feature, to explore the possibilities for its use within the network. This additional authentication method focuses on confirming that a person is genuinely human, while offering a different form of privacy protection, because it does not require showing one's face. For example, for Pioneers who already need multiple face liveness checks or re-confirmation of their identity information, the palm may be used as part of a wider authentication and security flow.

Possible future uses include use in identity verification for account recovery and protection, password resets, two-factor authentication (a two-step check), and other security-related uses.

This beta feature will soon be released to some Pioneers within Pi KYC, and will be evaluated further after that.

KYC validator reward distribution targets the first quarter of 2026

The distribution of validator rewards continues to progress, in line with the schedule announced before. Its design is final, the implementation is complete, and the work is now being tested. Testing is extremely important, because it affects the distribution of Pi payments to KYC validators for their work in the KYC processes of millions of Pioneers. The target remains rollout by the end of this quarter (March 2026).

As mentioned before, the first distribution of validator rewards requires a great deal of work to evaluate and analyze the huge amount of KYC validation task data built up since 2021. The reasons for the large amount of work are as follows.

  • The volume of task data: hundreds of millions of validation tasks at different stages
  • The complexity of the task data: differences in task types, results, and quality, plus tasks done at different stages of KYC processing (beta, testing, launch, and scaling stages)
  • Fairness of the rewards in the design of the distribution, and the source of the funds
  • The scalability (the ability to grow in size) of the system structure that now distributes these rewards to millions of validators, and its ability to support future reward cycles
  • The accuracy of the calculations, and the security of distributing these rewards on the Mainnet blockchain

These updates show the ongoing work to widen access to KYC and Mainnet migration and to enable wider participation in Pi's ecosystem. People are called on to complete the required KYC and Mainnet migration steps if needed, and to become KYC validators from today, to contribute to the progress of KYC in their own country or region.
