Pi’s Open Network Has Launched!

Published: February 19, 2025

Categories: announcement, general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

To all Pioneers, the Open Network (the live network that connects with the outside) has started. This means that Pi Network's external connection is now available for the Pi community around the world. The Open Network has opened Pi's active ecosystem (all the related apps and services together), innovative apps, and wide person-to-person network for integration with the wider blockchain world. The Open Network was key to Pi's mission to create a cryptocurrency that anyone can use, that includes everyone, that is driven by real uses, and that is widely used.

As stated in the announcement of the date of Pi Network's Open Network, the start on this day is an important milestone. It connects Pi Network, and the Pioneers who have moved Pi forward together over the past 6 years, with the wider world.

External connection is now working. Pioneers can use Pi's ecosystem and apps, which are supported by real uses of Pi, to connect with other networks and systems that follow the rules. Pioneers can also make transactions outside the Pi ecosystem. This expands Pi's existing uses and reach.

In addition, a list of businesses verified by KYB (Know Your Business, a check of a business) is available on a web page that Pioneers can view and access. It helps Pioneers identify businesses that have finished Pi Network's KYB review process. Businesses that are not on the list can apply for KYB verification from the linked page.

The Open Network also brings support for integration with centralized exchanges (CEXs, exchanges run by a central company) and onramps (services that are an entry point for changing regular money into cryptocurrency), to give additional ways to get Pi for use in the Pi ecosystem. It also brings more third-party services, Node updates, and more.

This article explains in detail the Open Network opportunities and releases that are now available, together with a video by one of the Pi founders, Chengdiao Fan. You can also get ready to take part in the upcoming Open Network Challenge, which will be held in the Pi mining app next week. It is a way to learn about and explore directly what the Open Network offers.

Connecting Pi Safely with External Systems

Under this heading, the article explains how to connect Pi safely with external systems.

Integration with Onramps and Centralized Exchanges (CEXs)

Pioneers can now connect their Pi for use with external systems and networks. CEX platforms and onramps integrated with Pi Network after they passed KYB. These KYB-verified third-party services help Pioneers connect with the wider crypto asset ecosystem (the world of digital assets) and the world of regular money, and the reverse is also true. You must not use any third-party service, including a CEX that claims it integrated with Pi but is not listed on the KYB services web page. If you do so, you run the risk of fraud. For details, see the section 'Compliance Requirements for Businesses' below.

Compliance Requirements for Businesses

As part of the work to keep a safe ecosystem that follows the rules, participation in Mainnet (the live network) blockchain activities requires KYC (Know Your Customer, an identity check) verification for Pioneers, and KYB (Know Your Business, a business check) verification for businesses. This requirement applies to companies (not individuals) that seek access to the Pi Mainnet, Mainnet Nodes, or other Mainnet-related services, and to companies that provide important infrastructure (basic systems that other services depend on), such as onramps and offramps (services for changing cryptocurrency back into regular money), exchanges, bridges (systems that connect different blockchains), and Web3 wallet solutions. The KYB process is meant to keep compliance with regulations and keep the ecosystem safe. Pi works to be a safe Web3 (a decentralized web based on blockchain) space. There, Pioneers can use external connections, use their Pi through them, and engage with KYB-verified businesses and partners that connect with real KYC-verified Pioneers, and the reverse is also true.

Only KYB-verified businesses are eligible to create individual non-custodial (a form where you manage your assets yourself) Mainnet Pi Wallets that let them make transactions in the Pi ecosystem. This extra layer of verification reduces the risk of dishonest activity and helps build trust among Pioneers. A legitimate and real business will have no problem passing KYB.

Businesses that want to integrate with Pi and finish KYB can do so from the linked page. By putting these standards in place, Pi Network improves the safety, legitimacy, and long life of the ecosystem, and establishes a verified base for future integration with businesses with a good reputation.

You can see a full list of all businesses that have finished KYB on the linked page. You need to be extremely careful about trading Pi with any business that is not listed on that page. Because there is a KYB requirement for access to Pi Mainnet wallets, there may be businesses that claim they can act as an intermediary for Pi-related business, even though they do not actually own a Pi Mainnet wallet. To avoid possible fraud, you must not engage with such businesses.

Node Release

With the Open Network, the firewall (a system that blocks connections from the outside) that was in place during the Enclosed Period (the period when the network was not connected to the outside) has been removed. Now, technically, anyone can add a Node (a computer that supports the network) to the Mainnet blockchain, in the sense of running the protocol (the rules for communication) and connecting to the network. Strong past contributions and reliability scores are given priority at first. Pioneers will be invited little by little to move their Nodes from Testnet (the test network) to Mainnet through the desktop Node screen. Information on Node rankings will be published soon.

Node 0.5.1 was recently released to the Pi community. The contents of this update are as follows.

  • It let users who have the Blockchain API service (a service that lets programs connect to the blockchain) turned on move their data after a protocol upgrade, through a new simple button on the screen
  • It established a consistent public key (a public code used to identify and verify) for all Nodes, to prepare for the posting of public Node rankings
  • It improved Node performance, reduced the load on the CPU (the part of a computer that does calculations), and made sure operations are smoother for Node operators

Next Week: Learn More and Explore Open Network Through an In-App Challenge

Next week, to celebrate the Open Network and to support the uses and possibilities that are now available, Pioneers can take part in a new Open Network Challenge. It lets them learn more about the Open Network and explore the ecosystem. It is an interactive way to explore the different parts of the Pi ecosystem and to contribute to the use of its different utilities, and to unlock fun rewards in the process.

In this Open Network Challenge, you complete 12 actions. They guide you to the new content released on this day, help you finish important individual steps, such as encouraging your team to do KYC and migrate to Mainnet, and celebrate through Pi's different social media apps, such as Pi Chats and Fireside Forum.
