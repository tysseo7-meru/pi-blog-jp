Pi2Day 2026: Advancing Utility and External Access Through Compute, AI, and Identity

Published: June 28, 2026

Categories: announcement, pi-day

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi Network celebrated Pi2Day and made new announcements that widen uses within the Pi ecosystem and, at the same time, widen uses to the world outside Pi. There are three main announcements this time. They offer Pi's services to companies and developers outside Pi, and invite them to join Pi Network and use Pi's unique advantages and resources.

  • SoloHost: an open system on Pi Desktop (the computer version of the Pi app) that anyone can join without permission. Developers can build and list apps that run on their own computer, and users can find and run those apps on their own computer. It is also useful for local AI (artificial intelligence that runs inside your own device) and for the soon-to-start distributed computing (sharing the power of many computers to do calculations). Users can also use the apps from a mobile device via Pi Browser (Pi's dedicated browser)
  • Pi Sign-in: a system that lets Pioneers (Pi users) log in to supported external websites and apps with their own Pi account. External services can receive the benefit of Pi's large number of users and its verified identity information resources
  • PiVerify: a service that provides Pi's "power to confirm that someone is a real human being" to outside clients who need KYC (identity verification, the procedure to confirm a customer's identity) for their users. Outside platforms can reduce fake accounts and duplicate accounts, and use it for procedures to follow the law. Clients pay the usage fee in Pi, so Pi's uses also increase

These three announcements advance Pi's practical uses in the fields of computing power, AI, and identity verification. By first providing useful services and resources to the outside — such as the blockchain (the technology that manages transaction records in a distributed way) foundation, identity verification, and a large network actively engaged around the world — Pi prepares several reasons not only to use Pi's services, but also to join and contribute to the Pi ecosystem.

This is especially relevant in the AI era. As AI continues to change how people, apps, and companies use digital foundations, computing power and identity verification are becoming ever more important as the base for apps to run, grow in scale, and serve real users. Apps need more computing resources, people become sensitive about the handling of personal information while receiving AI's benefits, demand grows for AI apps that run on their own devices and for connections between devices, and many digital services need better ways to confirm the participation of real humans.

In addition to these product updates, a Pi2Day Ecosystem Quest (an in-app event for learning about the new announcements) was prepared. The community can learn about the new announcements, try the features, take part in quizzes, and earn in-app badges to show each other.

SoloHost beta released: Pi Desktop, self-run apps, and distributed computing

Pi introduced SoloHost. It is a new system inside Pi Desktop, with an open listing process that outside developers can join without permission, where they can build and list their own apps and tools for nodes (computers taking part in the network). Users can find, check the contents of, install, and run these self-run apps on their own computers.

Apps suited to SoloHost are generally the following.

  • Apps for personal use. For example, AI agents (AI that does work on the user's behalf) that run on your own device
  • (Under development) Distributed computing apps that use available node resources

With this announcement, the existing Pi Node and Pi Desktop environment becomes a more practical foundation for creating uses. Instead of treating nodes only as the foundation supporting Pi's blockchain, Pioneers gain new ways to use their own computers for apps, AI tools, distributed computing, and other uses that need computing.

Because SoloHost is built on the existing Pi Node and Pi Desktop environment, the hurdle for running self-run software is lowered. Users do not need to set up servers or Docker environments (a package of systems for running apps) by hand, and access to familiar apps becomes easy through Pi Desktop. Also, because the Pioneer's computer itself becomes that person's server, they can access, from a mobile phone, the corresponding apps running on the desktop, through the SoloHost apps inside Pi Browser.

Through this system, people can receive AI's benefits while keeping their data on their own devices, without needing to hand data to outside remote servers.

SoloHost is currently released as an early beta (trial version), and the range where it becomes available will widen in stages. As product improvement advances, the experience and features may change greatly.

One of the apps published on SoloHost is Hermes. It is an open source (its design is public) local AI agent that runs while storing all data on the user's own computer. Hermes shows that a local AI agent can help complete work, answer questions, and work with information, while keeping the actual computing and data on the user's device. By running it on your own device, you can manage data and privacy more strongly, reduce dependence on outside foundations and cloud services for corresponding work, and directly use the computing power you already have.

Distributed computing is also another core of SoloHost. As AI models, agents, and apps grow, demand for computing resources keeps increasing, while much of the computing power available in the world remains distributed across individual devices. Pi's node network is in a different position. It is already a large-scale distributed network, made of more than 420,000 computers run by Pioneers, and if node operators choose to take part, they can cooperate for useful work.

By helping SoloHost-suitable apps access available node resources, Pi Nodes can become a practical computing layer for AI-related apps and other work that needs a lot of computing, and Pioneers gain a way to contribute directly to, and benefit from, these new production processes through the computers they already run.

As one example, there is a distributed computing app coming in the future, in which roughly the top 100 node operators can provide available computing resources to complete AI work. This app is built on an earlier proof-of-concept project (a prototype to check whether an idea holds), and it is a real app that further automates the functions, widens the scale, and completes them, aiming to provide practical-level uses to outside clients. Participating nodes can receive rewards in Pi from outside clients. This further shows that Pi Nodes can support Pi's uses beyond the blockchain foundation, by turning spare computing power into a resource for real apps. This app will be published on SoloHost right after Pi2Day.

For developers and publishers, SoloHost is designed as an open listing process for node-based tools that anyone can join without permission. Any developer with a Pi account can create a self-run app and apply for listing. There are structural checks on the package, but there is no pre-publication review flow like the traditional one. This makes the list closer to an open developer platform than a carefully selected app store, and it encourages participation especially from the AI developer community. Users must evaluate each app and package themselves, and install at their own responsibility.

Through this, developers gain a new way to reach more than 420,000 Pi Node operators who already run Pi Desktop and can run apps on their own devices, and they can also reach the even larger community of millions. Some in the community may also install Pi Desktop to receive the benefits of useful local AI agents.

  • If you are building a local AI agent or a self-run app, this content suggests considering a SoloHost listing. Node operators and Pioneers who need to run their own local AI for data privacy will be able to find, download, and run your app through Pi Desktop
  • If you need computing power for suitable, non-confidential work, this content says Pi will soon publish a practical case. It shows developers' ability to build distributed computing tools and connect their work with participating Pi Nodes

SoloHost developers can also benefit from the strengths of the Pi ecosystem, such as distribution, identity verification through Pi KYC, login with a Pi account, the Pi Wallet, Pi Browser, node setup, and familiarity with Docker.

As self-run apps and node-based tools become part of the Pi Desktop and wider ecosystem experience, ways to connect users, devices, publishers, and services also become necessary. Apps need a way to authenticate Pi users easily, a way to enable connections to users' local devices, a way to support access to the whole ecosystem experience, and a way to build on trusted participation. That is where Pi Sign-in comes in.

Pi Sign-in: connecting the internet with Pi's verified identity

With Pi Sign-in, supported external websites and apps can offer Pi as a login or new registration option. This becomes more relevant as Pi widens to more apps and tools outside the Pi ecosystem.

For users, it means that instead of creating a new account and password for each app, they can access supported services with their own Pi account. Depending on the experience, they may allow access through Pi by scanning a QR code, or by moving from a mobile browser to Pi Browser. All information shared from Pi to an app requires the user's explicit consent. This announcement is the first version of Pi Sign-in, and it will be improved repeatedly in the future.

The reason external services and websites want to build in Pi Sign-in is that Pi Sign-in connects Pi's large user base with those services more easily, and creates a way to welcome Pi users into their product experience. Also, beyond user inflow, it gives those services a way to use Pi Network's additional advantages and resources, while raising Pi's presence in more external settings.

This is especially important together with the SoloHost announcement. Even if an app runs locally on a Pioneer's desktop, it may need to support user access from another local device, such as their mobile phone, in order to receive the benefits of a local AI agent remotely. Pi Sign-in authenticates other local devices through the user's Pi account, and in passing also enables interaction with other Pi ecosystem services such as the wallet. Logging in with Pi is proof that the device is your own, and that only you can open the app. Because Pi Sign-in helps limit that access to the correct Pi user, AI apps running locally on a computer become useful and accessible also from devices other than the computer itself, and users do not need the technical work of setting up their own device network. Through this, the local AI experience becomes equal to the experience offered by external AI apps, without exposing personal information.

In the future, Pi Sign-in may also help developers build experiences that use the broader strengths of the Pi ecosystem, such as the resource of verified humans backed by KYC.

PiVerify: KYC and identity verification to confirm real human beings, for outside parties

Pi has started PiVerify. It is a KYC and identity verification service that lets outside clients use Pi's power to confirm real human beings. With PiVerify, outside platforms can verify their own users using Pi's existing KYC verification system, reduce fake and duplicate accounts, and support identity verification flows for following the law.

PiVerify was first introduced at the first anniversary of Pi Network's Open Network, and it is built on Pi's KYC model that combines AI and humans. This model has verified more than 18 million Pioneers in more than 200 countries and regions. The system combines automatic verification by AI with verification by humans, supporting the confirmation of real human beings at a worldwide scale. Providers offering KYC already exist, but Pi's method differs in the following points: its worldwide geographic range, its large scale, its verification model combining AI and humans, and the completeness of its solution. Pi KYC handles not only identity verification, but also sanctions checks for legal compliance, the human validator flow, comparisons across networks, and handling different regulatory forms.

By making this foundation available to the outside, PiVerify widens the uses of Pi's verified identity technology and resources beyond Pi's own apps and services. As more services use PiVerify, the total of people verified through the Pi ecosystem will grow beyond the 18 million KYC-verified Pioneers, further strengthening the foundation of verified humans behind the Pi ecosystem. Because outside clients pay in Pi for using the PiVerify service, Pi's own uses also widen.

For companies and platforms, the need is simple. Many digital services need to know whether their users are real people. Verified identity is necessary for meaningful transactions, especially transactions in the real-world economy.

Fintech (services combining finance and technology) platforms, exchanges, Web3 services, trust-based apps, and AI and data platforms all face the risks of fake accounts, duplicate users, fraud, nuisance behavior, and Sybil attacks (attacks that create a large number of fake identities). PiVerify is designed to help such services build real-human confirmation into registration, re-verification, access, participation, payments, and data flows.

PiVerify can support identity verification services such as document checks, liveness checks (confirming that the person is there in person), handling sanctions and AML (anti-money laundering) screening, and duplicate detection. These abilities help outside clients raise trust and reduce misuse, and give them the means to connect with PiVerify in flexible ways, such as building the verification flow directly into their own products.

In the original article, guidance was shown for businesses to learn more about PiVerify and try it.

Pi2Day Ecosystem Quest

To celebrate Pi2Day and the new features starting across the whole ecosystem, Pi introduced the Pi2Day 2026 Ecosystem Quest for Pioneers.

This year's Ecosystem Quest guides Pioneers to complete activities where they can discover new apps, try ecosystem features, and understand how Pi's foundation supports the creation of uses in the fields of computing power, AI, and identity verification.

Because some Quest steps require completing a quiz, attention to the details of this update is necessary. Pioneers who complete the Quest receive a special-edition in-app badge that can be displayed in Pi Chats and Pi Social Profiles.

Participation in the Ecosystem Quest is done by tapping the "Pi2Day" icon at the top right of the Pi mining app's home screen. The Quest ends on July 13.

Connecting computing power, identity verification, and community participation

Pi2Day 2026 shows that Pi's uses continue to grow through both foundation and participation. Pi Desktop and Nodes support new local apps and computing uses, Pi Sign-in helps connect those experiences among users, devices, and services, and PiVerify can widen Pi's power to confirm real human beings to outside clients.

Taken together, these announcements show the ecosystem's broader direction. Practical uses are built not only by product releases, but also by the Pioneers, node operators, developers, validators, app teams, merchants, and external services that use them, try them, and build on them. Also, Pi's products are not only for the Pi ecosystem; they also widen Pi's services and resources to the outside world, and that in turn enriches and strengthens the Pi ecosystem.
