AI Upgrades to Pi KYC Accelerate KYC Processing, Unblocking Mainnet Migration

Published: December 5, 2025

Categories: update

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Pi's standard KYC, which allows Pioneers to migrate to the Mainnet, now uses additional AI in its checking process. The AI uses the same base technology as Pi Fast Track KYC (a faster identity check).

With this upgrade, the standard KYC process became faster and easier to scale, while keeping its accuracy. Because of the AI, the queue of KYC applications waiting for a human validator to review and decide fell by 50%. This eases the bottleneck around validators and may shorten the processing time of standard KYC.

Pi Fast Track KYC was rolled out a few months ago for new Pioneers and people who had not used Pi before. It let them get a Mainnet wallet and take part in the Pi Mainnet system. Fast Track KYC itself does not enable Mainnet migration. After looking at how it was used over the past few months, Pi built its base technology into the standard KYC process, which clears the way for Mainnet migration.

By using AI more in all checks, this update also does the following.

  • It helps solve the shortage of validators in some countries and regions
  • It helps unblock more Pioneers, faster
  • It reduces the amount of checking that humans need to do, saving human resources across the network
  • It reduces the KYC application information shown to human validators (note that all sensitive data is already hidden)

This change not only lets applications move forward more efficiently, but also reduces the burden on validators without reducing their work opportunities. The AI checks are set to be very cautious on purpose, so they do not let applications pass by mistake. When a case is hard to judge, it is sent to a human validator for further checking and a decision. This is also meant to reduce wrong rejections by the AI (cases where an application should have passed but the AI turned it down).

Validators still have plenty of KYC checking work to complete. That work keeps supporting the network by verifying accounts and unblocking Pioneers. At the same time, the human labor saved overall can be directed further toward new work opportunities that come from uses of the system, such as human feedback for AI models and products based on reinforcement learning (a way of training AI by rewarding good results).

Overall, Pi's KYC system aims to provide identity verification that is accurate, easy to use, able to scale, and careful with privacy — not only for millions of Pioneers around the world, but also for outside services such as Web3 (a decentralized web built on blockchains) and AI, where verified identity and proof that a person is real are strongly needed.

These improvements also support the background of Pi's long-term idea of an identity-verified blockchain. Identity verification not only makes sure the network stays sound; it also supports the use and development of apps and use cases inside the system. It also makes it possible to turn the resources of the whole network into another foundational use case, ready to connect with services that need verification and use cases that need proof that a person is real.

Building AI into the checking process supports all of these aims by making the system faster, more consistent, and easier to scale.

Update on the distribution of validator rewards

Work toward distributing rewards for validators' work is continuing and making progress. The first distribution of validator rewards requires a large amount of work: the data on the huge volume of KYC checking work built up since 2021 has to be evaluated and analyzed. The amount of work is large for the following reasons.

  • The amount of work data: hundreds of millions of checking tasks at different stages
  • The complexity of the work data: differences in the types of work, results, and quality, plus work done at different stages of KYC processing (beta, testing, launch, and scaling stages)
  • Fairness of the rewards in the design of the distribution and its funding source
  • The structure of the system that now distributes these rewards to millions of validators must be able to handle the scale, and must also support future reward cycles
  • The accuracy of the calculations, and safety when these rewards are distributed on the Mainnet blockchain

This work is targeting a rollout by the end of the first quarter of 2026.

Updated numbers for KYC and Mainnet migration

Pi recently published statistics on how many Tentative KYC Pioneers were helped when their blocks were cleared by the new system process.

Note that about 3 million Tentative KYC Pioneers can still unblock themselves. To qualify as fully KYC'd, these Pioneers must submit an additional liveness check (a check that the person is really operating the app) in the app. If this applies to you, please complete the liveness check as soon as you can.

A brief update on the numbers of people who have fully passed KYC and migrated across the network is as follows.

  • 17.5 million Pioneers have fully passed KYC
  • 15.7 million Pioneers have migrated to the Mainnet

Pioneers who have fully passed KYC should complete the Mainnet Checklist and migrate to the Mainnet. This includes setting up and confirming a Mainnet wallet, completing two-factor authentication (2FA, a system that adds one more step of checking on top of the usual check) to protect the wallet, and signing the token acknowledgment agreement, among other steps.

These millions of KYC'd Pioneers on the Pi Mainnet, and the many more who will join, are an important result of the whole Pi community's efforts. They are a large resource for the network: they help keep the network safe and sound, and they form a base for growing real use cases in the Web3 and AI industries that will shape the future.
