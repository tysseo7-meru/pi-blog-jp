CiDi Games’ Beta Launch Demonstrates the Power of Building on Pi

Published: May 28, 2026

Categories: announcement

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

On May 14, CiDi Games launched a beta app (a test version of an app) in the Pi ecosystem (the set of Pi apps and services). It introduced an HTML5 game platform (a place for games made with web technology that you can play at once, with no need to install anything), available through the Pi Browser.

The beta launched with the first 10 games across the casual, puzzle, idle (games that progress while you leave them alone), action, and competitive categories. It also included skill-based tournaments, progress on the platform through CiDiScore, the Pi ELF companion experience, Elf Continent, and a base for developers to build games that work with Pi. Together, these features make CiDi Games a platform-level experience. It brings gameplay, identity, community participation, and developer opportunity into one environment that runs on Pi.

CiDi Games is a company that Pi Network Ventures has invested in. It gives Pioneers a new way to use Pi through games. At the same time, it widens the Pi ecosystem with a base that can support more games and developers over time.

Showing natural growth on Pi

In less than one week after launch, CiDi Games naturally drew more than 81,000 Pioneers from more than 160 countries and regions. That activity led to deeper involvement. It recorded more than 1.2 million game sessions (times a game was played) in total, and 21,000 competition and tournament participants.

CiDi Games also recorded a large number of ads shown. This shows that, as long as creators build useful and engaging apps, apps on Pi can start to earn money with real users through the Pi Ad Network (Pi's advertising system).

The app ranking of CiDi Games in Ecosystem Directory Staking (staking in the list of Pi apps) was pushed up by a total of 3.19 million Pi. Most of that was staked (Pi put in to support an app) by the community, and CiDi Games became one of the Pi apps with the most Pi staked on it. Staking is an optional way for Pioneers and businesses to support the apps they create, use, or enjoy in the Ecosystem Interface (the screen that lists Pi apps). It also gives developers a visible sign of community interest.

These one-week results were completely natural. They were not created by promotion from Pi's official accounts. Pioneers found CiDi Games and took part entirely on their own. This shows that a well-made app that fits real user needs can find meaningful involvement through the Pi ecosystem, with no extra cost to gain users.

What the CiDi Games beta launch shows about the Pi platform and building on Pi

The natural growth of the CiDi Games beta launch shows Pi's answer to a growing problem for developers in general. Building apps is becoming easier, but reaching real users is becoming harder.

The Pi platform connects developers and creators with a large, active community of Pioneers. It supports app experiences that solve human needs.

For developers and creators, the Pi ecosystem is not only a place to publish an app. It is also a platform where real people can find, use, support, and improve an app. The CiDi Games beta launch showed that Pi's base can support products that work in both directions, while giving developers access to a community of 60M+ (more than 60 million) active Pioneers, ways to take part that are specific to Pi, and tools to earn money that can help them improve their products and keep developing.

Any developer can gain from what the Pi ecosystem offers by building and publishing a Pi app that solves real human needs. You submit an application through the Pi Developer Portal on the Pi Browser.

Creators who use outside AI tools, such as Codex, Claude Code, Replit, Cursor, Lovable, and other AI-assisted coding tools (tools that help write programs with AI), can easily change their apps into Pi apps through a developer tool recently released in Pi App Studio. There, you can use ready-made AI prompts (written instructions for AI) to add the Pi SDK (a set of development tools for connecting to Pi), check the setup, and add Pi payments. Pi App Studio also helps publish an app into the Pi ecosystem, as CiDi Games did, and makes it easy to get ready to gain users and have them take part.

In addition to growth and involvement, Pi apps are already connected with other base parts of Pi Network. These are global payments, Pi Wallet, Pi Ad Network, and access to the social network.

Continuing app development and integration in the Pi ecosystem

Working with Pi, CiDi Games will keep gathering opinions, improve the user experience, refine the platform features, and widen its range of games. Real use gives developers ongoing opinions about what works well, what can be improved, and where new uses can appear.

Readers were asked to open CiDi Games in the Pi Browser, try the beta app, play the games that you can play at once, and explore a new gaming experience that runs on Pi.
