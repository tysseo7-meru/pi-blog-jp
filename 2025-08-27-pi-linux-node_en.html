Linux Node Release and Upcoming Protocol Upgrades

Published: August 27, 2025

Categories: announcement, general

Original article

This page is a plain-English summary of an article from the official Pi Network blog. It is not a full translation. Difficult words and expressions are explained in simple words. For the exact wording, see the original article.

Release of the Linux Node

The Pi ecosystem has released the Linux version of Node (a node is a program that supports the network). Node is the backbone of Pi's distributed foundation. Expanding Node support beyond Mac and Windows makes the network stronger in resilience (the ability to recover) and adaptability.

For partners and services, including exchanges, the release of the Linux Node has immediate meaning. Many of these organizations already run their own Linux-based Nodes to connect to Pi. With the release of the official Linux Node, partners and services can move to standardized Node software. The main benefit here is that Linux Node operators can manage protocol updates themselves, or enable automatic updates from Pi Network, and keep up to date with future versions without difficulty. This replaces the current dependence on custom-built setups and creates a more stable and consistent system.

For Pioneers, the Linux Node is a feature the community has frequently requested over the past years. Because it is not directly connected to Node rewards, it may not affect daily participation right away, but this update widens ease of use and participation in the system. Its meaning lies in the foundation. By supporting Linux, the network becomes more open to developers, technically skilled Pioneers, and people who want to contribute through open source (publicly available designs) environments.

The installation steps can be checked in the official guide.

The upgrade to protocol version 23 is coming

Alongside the Linux Node release, Pi Network is preparing an important update: the move of the protocol (the rules for communication) from version 19 to version 23. Pi's protocol is built based on Stellar's protocol. This version is Pi's own protocol, built on a foundation that takes in updates from Stellar protocol version 23, and it enables a new level of functions and control. Future protocol updates will come in step by step through consecutive versions, and planned pauses of the blockchain service may be needed. Announcing such updates in advance is meant to help partners, external services, and the community prepare ahead of time.

The rollout will be done in stages, for careful testing.

  • The Testnet1 (test network 1) update begins this week and will continue over the coming weeks. Note that during the Testnet1 protocol update period, small pauses may occur on the Testnet1 blockchain. Pi will distribute a new community Node container for Testnet1, and there is no action users need to take.
  • In the weeks after the Testnet1 update is complete, Testnet2 and Mainnet (the production network) will come in stages, and the whole system will become version 23. Note that individual pauses may occur during the update period, and these will be announced to partners and Pioneers in advance. On the sites of external services such as centralized exchanges (CEX, exchanges run by a company), there may also be short pauses in the use of Pi to handle this update.

A blockchain that has received KYC (identity verification) opens up additional uses in lawful exchanges between users. Pi Network continues to operate as one of the leading KYC-verified blockchains, with tens of millions of KYC-completed accounts, but the way that verification is enforced is changing. With the protocol update, this enforcement will be done directly by the blockchain protocol itself. Importantly, this update also makes it possible to distribute KYC authority.

Pi's own KYC system will continue to verify Pioneers, but in the future the protocol will also make it possible to assign KYC authority to other trusted organizations. This provides a more distributed, community-led KYC flow, while keeping legal compliance at the protocol level.

To be clear, identity verification through KYC is important for the health of the network, for legal compliance, and for readiness for integration by services that require identity verification. Pi's early idea from years ago of building a network of a KYC-verified blockchain, and its achievement of more than 14.82 million people who have migrated and been KYC-verified on the Pi Mainnet blockchain, are now also reflected in recent industry moves toward verified, permission-based standards such as ERC-3643, which build identity verification and legal-compliance systems directly into tokens on public networks.
